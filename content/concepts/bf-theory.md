## Definition

**BF theory** is the topological field theory of a 1-form gauge field $A$ and a $(d-2)$-form $B$ in $d$ spacetime dimensions, with the action built from the wedge product alone,
$$
S = \frac{iN}{2\pi}\int B\wedge dA ,
$$
in the Euclidean normalization of the generalized-symmetries course, where $N$ is an integer **level**. No metric enters, so the theory is topological. The two gauge redundancies are $A\to A+d\lambda$ and $B\to B+d\mu$, the equations of motion are $dA=0$ and $dB=0$, and the solutions are flat connections: there are no local degrees of freedom.

What the theory does carry is a set of extended operators and the phases they see. The Wilson line $e^{iq\oint A}$ and the surface operator $e^{i\oint B}$ link, and the linking phase is $e^{2\pi i q/N}$, which is the Aharonov–Bohm phase of a charge $q$ around a unit of discrete flux. That phase is the whole content: **level-$N$ BF theory is the continuum form of $\mathbb{Z}_N$ gauge theory**, with an electric $\mathbb{Z}_N^{(1)}$ symmetry generated by the surfaces and acting on the lines, and a magnetic $\mathbb{Z}_N^{(2)}$ acting on the surfaces (in four dimensions; see [[higher-form-symmetries]] for the degree counting in general $d$).

Two facts about the level are worth keeping in front:

- **The level counts the ground states.** On a spatial three-torus the four-dimensional theory has $N^3$ ground states, one for each class in $H^1(T^3,\mathbb{Z}_N)$; in three dimensions the count on a genus-$g$ surface is $N^{2g}$. This degeneracy is [[topological-order|topological order]], and it is what the [[toric-code|toric code]] realizes at $N=2$ — the toric code is $\mathbb{Z}_2$ BF theory, the $K$-matrix $\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\2&0\end{pmatrix}$ of the [[chern-simons-theory|Chern–Simons]] description.
- **Level one is trivial.** At $N=1$ the degeneracy is $1$ on any spatial topology and no line or surface carries a nontrivial phase. A level-one BF term is therefore not a topologically ordered phase; it is an invertible theory, and reading it as "topological order" is a mistake that costs the distinction between a gapped phase with a degeneracy and one without.

In three dimensions BF theory is the doubled Chern–Simons theory $U(1)_N\times U(1)_{-N}$, which is why it is non-chiral and why [[string-net-condensation|string-net]] constructions produce it rather than the chiral states of the [[quantum-hall-effect|quantum Hall effect]].

## Role in Research

BF theory is where the group's [[confinement-duality]] line meets the modern language of generalized symmetries.

- **The electric Julia–Toulouse condensate.** Condensing electric charges and applying the [[julia-toulouse-mechanism|Julia–Toulouse]] prescription produces an abelian BF term built on the *original* photon, at level one and accompanied by the Maxwell and $|dB|^2$ kinetic terms (Grigorio, Guimaraes, Rougemont, Wotzasek, Zarro, *Phys. Rev. D* 86 (2012) 027705). The same paper includes the magnetic defects consistently and obtains Dirac's veto from the brane symmetry. Because the level is one, what the construction delivers is the massive sector — integrating out $B$ gives the Proca theory of *Phys. Lett. B* 723 (2013) 422 — and not topological order.
- **What a charge-$N$ condensate would give.** Condensing matter of charge $N$ instead should leave a level-$N$ BF term behind, and with it a $\mathbb{Z}_N^{(1)}$ remnant of the electric symmetry. Working out how the level is fixed by the charge of the condensate is an open problem of the generalized-symmetries course (Sem I Week 11, problem 6⋆⋆) and the natural sharpening of the 2012 result.
- **The magnetic condensate is the mirror image.** There the Gaussian dual of the massive Kalb–Ramond form is a level-one BF term built on the **dual** photon. Which photon the $B$ field couples to is what distinguishes the two condensates.
- **Higher gauging.** The surfaces of BF theory are the symmetry operators that higher gauging sums over, so [[condensation-defects|condensation defects]] and the group's manuscript on higher-gauging walls live directly on top of this structure.

## Relations

- [[julia-toulouse-mechanism]] — the electric condensate produces a level-one BF term; the magnetic one produces it for the dual photon
- [[topological-order]] — level-$N$ BF theory is the continuum description of abelian topological order; level one carries none
- [[toric-code]] — the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ case, solved on the lattice
- [[chern-simons-theory]] — in three dimensions BF is the doubled theory $U(1)_N\times U(1)_{-N}$; the $K$-matrix language is shared
- [[higher-form-symmetries]] — the electric $\mathbb{Z}_N^{(1)}$ and magnetic symmetries of BF theory, and the degree counting in general $d$
- [[condensation-defects]] — the surfaces of BF theory are what higher gauging sums over
- [[lattice-gauge-theory]], [[villain-action]] — $\mathbb{Z}_N$ gauge theory on the lattice, of which this is the continuum limit
- [[axionic-electrodynamics]] — integrating out $B$ or $A$ in the Maxwell–BF system relates the massive and the axionic descriptions
- [[confinement]] — a cautionary relation: BF theory describes a **deconfined** phase, with perimeter-law Wilson lines, and not a confining one

## Notes

- The level is the only datum: two BF theories with the same $N$ are the same theory, and $N$ is quantized because the linking phase must be single-valued.
- BF theory has a local Lagrangian but is **not** a local net of type III₁ algebras in the sense of the [[haag-kastler-axioms|Haag–Kastler axioms]], since it has no local observables to speak of. That is the distinction [[2026-benedetti-magic-in-qft|the magic paper]] uses to separate local QFT from topological field theory, and the reason a BF ground state can be a stabilizer state while a QFT vacuum cannot.
