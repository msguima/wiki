## Definition

A **higher-form (generalized global) symmetry** is a symmetry whose charged objects are extended rather than pointlike. The organizing idea (Gaiotto–Kapustin–Seiberg–Willett, 2015) is that a symmetry is a **topological operator**: a $p$-form symmetry is generated by operators $U_g(M^{d-p-1})$ supported on closed codimension-$(p+1)$ submanifolds, and it acts on $p$-dimensional charged operators by linking in spacetime.

- $p = 0$ recovers ordinary symmetry: codimension-1 operators acting on local operators.
- $p = 1$ (a **1-form symmetry**) has codimension-2 operators acting on line operators (Wilson/'t Hooft lines).
- For $p \ge 1$ the symmetry group is necessarily **abelian**.

The framework gives the Landau paradigm back for phases with no local order parameter: a phase is characterized by which higher-form symmetries are realized, spontaneously broken, or anomalous. Confinement is the statement that a 1-form symmetry is **unbroken** ([[wilson-loop]] area law); the deconfined/Coulomb phase is its **spontaneous breaking** (perimeter law), with the photon as the Goldstone mode.

## Role in Research

Higher-form symmetry is the language that connects the group's historical [[confinement-duality]] work to the current research frontier. Every Semester I object of the generalized-symmetries course — center symmetry, the magnetic shift symmetry of compact QED, monopole and vortex conservation — is a higher-form symmetry; the Julia–Toulouse/higher-gauging manuscript is a statement about **gauging a higher-form symmetry on a hypersurface** (see [[condensation-defects]]).

- **Gauging** a discrete $p$-form symmetry (summing over backgrounds) produces a dual "quantum" $\mathbb{Z}_N^{(d-p-2)}$ symmetry — the rule behind [[kramers-wannier-duality]] and behind the global-form ambiguity ($SU(N)$ vs $SU(N)/\mathbb{Z}_N$).
- **Spontaneous breaking** of a 1-form symmetry is the sharp definition of a deconfined phase and of [[topological-order]] (ground-state degeneracy = broken 1-form symmetry).
- **Anomalies** of higher-form symmetries (e.g. Yang–Mills at $\theta=\pi$) constrain the phase diagram; see [[t-hooft-anomaly]].

## Notes

- "Symmetry = topological operator" is the whole reframing; conservation of a Noether current is the special case where the operator is codimension-1.
- The abelianness for $p \ge 1$ is geometric: two codimension-$\ge 2$ operators can be moved past each other, so their product cannot depend on order.
- Beyond invertible higher-form symmetries lie non-invertible ones (fusion is a projector, no inverse); these appear at self-dual points and as [[condensation-defects]].
