## Statement

Problem 2.2 of [[2026-goto-rethinking-qi-gravity-fields|Goto et al.]]: a quantum computer in a bulk subregion performs a task; by holographic duality the same task must be implementable on the boundary. What does bulk causal structure imply for boundary tasks, and conversely? The paper points to the **connected wedge theorem** relating bulk causal contact to preshared correlations and nonlocality on the boundary.

## Why It Matters

Peripheral to the group's current lines, but adjacent to the [[holographic-bell-program]]: the connected-wedge theorem is a statement about *nonlocality/entanglement required for a boundary task*, which is the same currency as Bell-CHSH. A long-run bridge, not a near-term calculation.

## What We Know

- The connected wedge theorem (May et al.) ties bulk causal features to boundary entanglement needed to complete a task with prescribed inputs/outputs.
- Bulk causality is encoded in boundary micro-causality plus the holographic dictionary.

## Possible Approaches

- Read connected-wedge-type constraints as Bell/nonlocality statements on boundary subalgebras.
- Relate to $f$-routing / non-local quantum computation, which already surfaced in the [[embezzlement-capacity-project]].
