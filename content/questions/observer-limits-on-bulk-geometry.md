## Statement

Problem 3.3 of [[2026-goto-rethinking-qi-gravity-fields|Goto et al.]]: once a reference structure (frame, observer algebra, reference state, code subspace) is fixed, what are the fundamental limits — information-theoretic *and* computational — on what an observer can extract about the bulk geometry from the microscopic state?

## Why It Matters

Peripheral to the group, but it is the complexity-theoretic counterpart of the group's [[embezzlement-capacity-lower-bound|capacity]] questions: both ask what is *operationally accessible* given a bounded agent. Harlow–Hayden / Python's-Lunch hardness is the black-hole-interior version.

## What We Know

- Harlow–Hayden: decoding the interior mode of an old black hole is exponentially complex.
- Python's Lunch: a geometric obstruction (non-minimal QES) signals exponential reconstruction complexity.
- State-dependent (Papadodimas–Raju) reconstruction has its own accessibility limits.

## Possible Approaches

- Separate information-theoretic accessibility (is it *there*?) from computational accessibility (can a bounded observer *get* it?).
- Relate complexity obstructions to bulk geometric features (QES structure).
