# Conventions

Notation for the [[courses/generalized-symmetries-course/syllabus|generalized-symmetries course]]. Read before Week 1; the cochain conventions below appear in every problem set. This file is the single source of every sign and normalization the notes use. **No note fixes a convention locally:** a note that needs something missing here adds it here, in the same commit (template §5). Where this file is silent on a matter no note uses yet, the default references are Gorantla–Lam–Seiberg–Shao (GLSS, arXiv:2103.01257) for Villain and modified-Villain constructions and Chen–Tata (arXiv:2106.05274) for higher cup products.

**Course-local override.** In this course, lowercase $d$ is the **Euclidean spacetime dimension** (the lattice-literature convention), so a $d$-dimensional theory has $D=d-1$ spatial directions in its Hamiltonian presentation. This differs from the owner's global default ($d$ = spatial dimensions), and the difference is the probable origin of erratum E1 (a $(d-2)$ rule for spatial $d$ evaluated at spacetime $d$); every degree formula in this course is therefore written with $d$ = spacetime dimension.

**Reserved symbols.**
- $d$: the spacetime dimension, and the coboundary operator on cochains; context always separates the two. A greatest common divisor is written $\gcd(N,q)$, or $r=\gcd(N,q)$ when a short symbol is needed, never $d$ (the group's manuscript, version of 2026-09-20, writes $g=\gcd(N,k)$ and uses $d$ for another greatest common divisor; quotations from it translate $g\to r$).
- $a$: the $U(1)$ lattice gauge field (a real 1-cochain) in the gauge weeks; in the 2d scalar weeks (Weeks 1–4) $a(x)=G(0)-G(x)$ is the subtracted Green function of §2, always written with its argument. The two never occur in one formula. In the RG of Week 4, $a$ without an argument is the running short-distance cutoff, rescaled as $a\to a\,e^{\ell}$. The **lattice spacing** is $a_{\rm lat}$ ($a_t$, $a_s$ on anisotropic lattices), set to 1 unless dimensional analysis is the point; in Weeks 1–4, where the letter $a$ is taken by the Green function, the same quantity is written $a_0$, as in $\ln(r/a_0)$.
- $\beta$: the inverse coupling of a lattice action; an inverse temperature only when a transfer matrix says so explicitly. $\beta_R$ is the renormalized (screened) stiffness of the BKT analysis.
- $G$: a lattice Green function. The spin–spin correlator is $C(x)=\langle e^{i(\theta_x-\theta_0)}\rangle$, always with its argument; in Week 4, $C$ without an argument is the invariant of the Kosterlitz flow.
- $e$: the $U(1)$ gauge coupling; $g$: the non-abelian and Hamiltonian coupling. For $U(1)$ the Kogut–Susskind $g$ equals $e$.
- $K$: context-dependent, and each note says which: the Ising coupling (Weeks 4–5), the plaquette coupling of the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Hamiltonians (Weeks 7 and 13–15), and the dual-photon stiffness $K\equiv e^2/8\pi^2$ of compact QED₃ (Week 10; §5).
- κ: the 2d Green-function constant of §2 in Weeks 1–4; the matter hopping coupling in Weeks 13–14, and the matter stiffness of the Villain abelian-Higgs model of Sem II Week 3; $\kappa_a$, always with its label, the Frobenius–Schur sign of Sem II Week 11. In Sem II Week 14, following the group's manuscript (§7), κ is the cost per unit length of a wall current, $\tilde\kappa=4\pi^2/\kappa N'^2$ the dual clock stiffness and $\beta_V=1/\kappa$ its Villain form, and the matter stiffness is written $\kappa_m$. $N'=N/r$ with $r=\gcd(N,k)$, and $\bar\alpha=\min_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|\alpha+N'm|$ is the minimal lift of $\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{N'}$. $\tau^{x,z}_v$: Pauli operators of ℤ₂ matter on sites (Week 13); τ alone is Euclidean time or the modular parameter of §10, as the context says.

## 1. Lattice and cell complex

- Hypercubic lattice $\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^d$. Euclidean signature throughout Semester I; Hamiltonian (continuous-time) presentations name their spatial lattice explicitly.
- A $p$-cell is $c=(x;S)$ with $x$ a site and $S=\{\mu_1<\dots<\mu_p\}$ a set of directions: sites are 0-cells, links $\ell_\mu(x)=(x;\{\mu\})$ run from $x$ to $x+\hat\mu$, plaquettes $P_{\mu\nu}(x)$ ($\mu<\nu$) are oriented by $(\hat\mu,\hat\nu)$, and so on. We write $\hat e_S=\sum_{\mu\in S}\hat\mu$.
- $p$-chains are formal integer combinations of $p$-cells, with boundary
$$
\partial(x;S)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{i-1}\Big[(x+\hat\mu_i;\,S\setminus\mu_i)-(x;\,S\setminus\mu_i)\Big],\qquad \partial^2=0 .
$$
  For a plaquette this is bottom + right − top − left: $\partial P_{\mu\nu}(x)=\ell_\mu(x)+\ell_\nu(x+\hat\mu)-\ell_\mu(x+\hat\nu)-\ell_\nu(x)$.
- $p$-cochains with coefficients in an abelian group $G$: $C^p(\Lambda,G)$, the $G$-valued functions on oriented $p$-cells. The coboundary is $(df)(c)=f(\partial c)$, so $d^2=0$; this *is* the discrete Stokes theorem, by definition. For a 0-cochain $(df)(\ell_\mu(x))=f(x+\hat\mu)-f(x)$; for a 1-cochain $(d\alpha)(P_{\mu\nu}(x))=\alpha_\mu(x)+\alpha_\nu(x+\hat\mu)-\alpha_\mu(x+\hat\nu)-\alpha_\nu(x)$.
- Cohomology $H^p(\Lambda,G)=\ker d/\operatorname{im}d$; on the torus $T^d$ computed by hand in Week 2. Coefficients used in the course: $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{R}$, $U(1)$, $\mathbb{Z}_N$.

## 2. Inner product, codifferential, Laplacian, dual lattice

- **Inner product.** $\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{p\text{-cells}}f(c)\,g(c)$ on real cochains; $\|f\|^2=\langle f,f\rangle$.
- **Codifferential.** $\delta:C^p\to C^{p-1}$ is the adjoint of $d$ under this inner product, with no further sign: $\langle\delta f,g\rangle=\langle f,dg\rangle$. On a 1-cochain,
$$
(\delta m)_x=\sum_\mu\big[m_\mu(x-\hat\mu)-m_\mu(x)\big]\qquad(\text{inflow minus outflow at }x).
$$
  The physical divergence is therefore $(\operatorname{div}m)_x=-(\delta m)_x$.
- **Laplacian.** $\Delta=\delta d+d\delta\ge0$. On 0-cochains $(\Delta f)_x=\sum_\mu\big(2f_x-f_{x+\hat\mu}-f_{x-\hat\mu}\big)$; the lattice second difference is $-\Delta$. The spin-wave action is $\frac\beta2\|d\theta\|^2=\frac\beta2\langle\theta,\Delta\theta\rangle$.
- **Green functions.** $\Delta G=\delta_{x,0}$ on the infinite lattice; on a finite torus $G'$ denotes the inverse of $\Delta$ on the complement of the constants. In Fourier space
$$
G(x)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}\,\frac{e^{ik\cdot x}}{\hat k^2},\qquad \hat k^2=\sum_\mu4\sin^2\tfrac{k_\mu}{2},
$$
  and the free scalar propagator is $\langle\theta_x\theta_y\rangle=G(x-y)/\beta$.
  - $d=2$: $G(0)$ diverges in the infrared; the subtracted function is finite,
$$
a(x)=G(0)-G(x)=\frac1{2\pi}\ln|x|+\kappa+O(|x|^{-2}),\qquad \kappa=\frac{2\gamma_E+\ln8}{4\pi}=0.257343\ldots,
$$
  with $a(1,0)=\tfrac14$ and $a(1,1)=1/\pi$ exactly.
  - $d=3$: $G(0)=0.252731\ldots$, $G(x)\to1/(4\pi|x|)$.
  - $d=4$: $G(0)=0.154933\ldots$, $G(x)\to1/(4\pi^2|x|^2)$.
- **Nearest-neighbour Green functions.** $G_d(0)-G_d(\hat e)=1/2d$ on the infinite hypercubic lattice, so $G_3(\hat e)=G_3(0)-\tfrac16$ and $G_4(\hat e)=G_4(0)-\tfrac18$.
- **Sums over a sublattice (Sem II Week 3 §4).** Summing $G_d$ over the translates of a point along $p$ of the $d$ lattice directions gives the Green function of the transverse displacement, $\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^p}G_d(x_\perp+k)=G_{d-p}(x_\perp)$, for $d-p\ge3$; it follows from the Bessel representation of §5, since $\sum_kI_k(2t)=e^{2t}$.
- **Dual lattice.** $\Lambda^*$ has a site at the center of every $d$-cell of $\Lambda$. The dual of the $p$-cell $(x;S)$ is the $(d-p)$-cell of $\Lambda^*$ based at $x+\tfrac12\hat e_S-\tfrac12\hat e_{S^c}$ and spanned by the complementary directions $S^c$ in increasing order.
- **Hodge map.** $\star:C^p(\Lambda)\to C^{d-p}(\Lambda^*)$ transports values with the sign of the shuffle,
$$
(\star f)\big((x;S)^*\big)=\epsilon(S,S^c)\,f\big((x;S)\big),
$$
  where $\epsilon(S,S^c)$ is the sign of the permutation that sorts $(S,S^c)$. The same rule maps $\Lambda^*$ back to $\Lambda$. With it, exactly as in the continuum,
$$
\star\star=(-1)^{p(d-p)},\qquad \delta=(-1)^{d(p+1)+1}\,\star d\,\star\quad\text{on }C^p,
$$
  checked cell by cell on random cochains for all $p$ in $d=2,3,4$. In $d=2$ the rule rotates a link by $+90°$ (the dual of $\ell_1(x)$ runs from $x+(\tfrac12,-\tfrac12)$ to $x+(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, the dual of $\ell_2(x)$ from $x+(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ to $x+(-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$), sends a site to the counter-clockwise dual plaquette around it, and gives $\delta=-\star d\star$ on 1-cochains, i.e. $(d\star m)(x^*)=\text{out}-\text{in}=-(\delta m)_x$ (Week 2 §5.2).
- Poincaré duality $H^p(\Lambda)\cong H_{d-p}(\Lambda)$ is used freely after Week 2.

- **Hodge map and intersection pairing (Sem II Week 1 §5).** $\star^{-1}\tilde\delta=(-1)^{d+q+1}\,d\,\star^{-1}$ on $C^q(\Lambda^*)$, and for chains $X$ of Λ and $\tilde Y$ of $\Lambda^*$ of complementary dimensions, with $X$ a $(p+1)$-chain, $I(\partial X,\tilde Y)=(-1)^{p+1}I(X,\partial\tilde Y)$. Together they give $d\lambda_{\tilde V}=\star^{-1}J_{\partial\tilde V}$ for the $\lambda_{\tilde V}$ of §6.
## 3. Poisson resummation and the Villain identities

The duality "move" of the course is always the same pair: (i) Poisson resummation of an integer cochain, (ii) solving the resulting constraint on the dual lattice. The identities, with the normalizations used everywhere:
$$
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)=\sum_{w\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\,f(x)\,e^{2\pi iwx},\qquad
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-2\pi ibn}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(b-m),
$$
$$
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\frac\beta2(\phi-2\pi n)^2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\beta}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\frac{m^2}{2\beta}+im\phi},\qquad
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\,e^{ik\theta}=\delta_{k,0}\ (k\in\mathbb{Z}).
$$
The last identity is why the integration over a compact variable must run over one period: integrating an angle over $\mathbb{R}$ while also summing its integer branch label overcounts by the branch redundancy and gives a divergent measure.

## 4. Compact fields, lattice actions and Hamiltonians

- **Compact scalar.** $\theta_x\in(-\pi,\pi]$. Villain action $S=\frac\beta2\|d\theta-2\pi n\|^2$ with $n\in C^1(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})$, redundancy $\theta\to\theta+2\pi k$, $n\to n+dk$. Vorticity $v=dn\in C^2(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})$. Since $d(d\theta-2\pi n)=-2\pi v$, the branch-reduced angle winds by $-2\pi v_P$ around a plaquette $P$: a counter-clockwise $+2\pi$ vortex has $v_P=-1$.
- **Compact gauge field.** $a_\ell\in(-\pi,\pi]$, measure $\prod_\ell\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}da_\ell/2\pi$. Villain action $S=\frac\beta2\|da-2\pi n\|^2$ with $n\in C^2(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})$, redundancies $a\to a+d\lambda$ and $a\to a+2\pi k$, $n\to n+dk$ ($k\in C^1(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})$). Monopole number $m=dn\in C^3(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})$; the flux of the Villain field strength out of a cube $c$ is $\sum_{\partial c}(da-2\pi n)=-2\pi m_c$.
- **Couplings and continuum limits.**
  - $U(1)$: Wilson action $S=\beta\sum_P\big(1-\cos(da)_P\big)$ or the Villain action above, with $\beta=1/(e^2a_{\rm lat}^{4-d})$; both reduce to $\frac1{2e^2}\int F\wedge\star F=\frac1{4e^2}\int F_{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}$, and $e^2$ has mass dimension $4-d$. The $SU(N)$ formula below is never applied to $U(1)$.
  - $SU(N)$: $S=\frac\beta N\sum_P\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(1-U_P)$ with $\beta=2N/(g^2a_{\rm lat}^{4-d})$ and $\operatorname{tr}T^aT^b=\tfrac12\delta^{ab}$; the continuum limit is $\frac1{2g^2}\int\operatorname{tr}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}=\frac1{4g^2}\int F^a_{\mu\nu}F^a_{\mu\nu}$. Links are $U_\ell=P\exp\big(i\int_\ell A\big)$ with earlier points to the left, so that $U_P=\exp\big(ia_{\rm lat}^2F_{\mu\nu}+O(a_{\rm lat}^3)\big)$ with $F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu+i[A_\mu,A_\nu]$ (coupling absorbed in $A$). The owner's continuum papers write $F^a_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A^a_\nu-\partial_\nu A^a_\mu+gf^{abc}A^b_\mu A^c_\nu$, the same convention after $A\to-gA$.
  - $\mathbb{Z}_2$ (Wegner): $S=-\beta\sum_P\sigma_P$, $\sigma_P=\prod_{\ell\in\partial P}\sigma_\ell$.
- **Character expansion.** $e^{-S_P(U)}=a_0(\beta)\big[1+\sum_{r\ne0}d_r\,\tilde c_r(\beta)\,\chi_r(U)\big]$ with $\tilde c_r=a_r/(d_ra_0)$ and $a_r=\int dU\,\chi_r(U)^*e^{-S_P(U)}$. At strong coupling $\langle W_r(C)\rangle=\tilde c_r^{\,A(C)}(1+\dots)$ and $\sigma_r=-\ln\tilde c_r$ is the leading area coefficient; for large loops, representations of zero $N$-ality are screened (the adjoint tube, Week 6 §7.5). With the Wilson action $\tilde c_r=O(\beta^{n_r})$, where $n_r$ is the minimal number of factors $F$ and $\bar F$ whose product contains $r$; $n_r$ equals the number of boxes of the Young diagram for $SU(2)$ and for representations first reached through $F^{\otimes k}$, and the adjoint of $SU(N)$ has $n=2$. Values: $U(1)$, $\tilde c_n=I_n(\beta)/I_0(\beta)$; $SU(2)$, $\tilde c_{1/2}=I_2(\beta)/I_1(\beta)=\beta/4+O(\beta^3)$ (the fundamental is pseudo-real, so $\operatorname{tr}U$ and $\operatorname{tr}U^\dagger$ both feed it); $SU(N\ge3)$, $\tilde c_F=\beta/2N^2+O(\beta^2)$.
- **Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonians** ($a_s=1$).
  - $U(1)$: $H=\frac{g^2}2\sum_\ell E_\ell^2-\frac1{g^2}\sum_P\cos\theta_P$, with $E_\ell\in\mathbb{Z}$, $[E_\ell,U_{\ell'}]=\delta_{\ell\ell'}U_\ell$, $U_\ell=e^{i\theta_\ell}$, and $g=e$. It is the $a_t\to0$ limit of the anisotropic Wilson action with $\beta_t=1/(g^2a_t)$, $\beta_s=a_t/g^2$, and gives photons of unit speed. Gauss law: $\sum_\mu[E_\mu(x)-E_\mu(x-\hat\mu)]=q_x$, i.e. $\delta E=-q$. Here $E_\ell$ is the momentum conjugate to the link angle; with the $a=-\varphi\,dt+A\cdot dx$ of §10 it is minus the physical field, $E_\ell\leftrightarrow-E/e^2$, so the Kogut–Susskind charge $q_x$ is minus the physical charge of §10: the static Wilson line of a charge $+1$, running forward in time, carries $q_x=-1$ (Week 7 §4.2; Sem II Week 1 §5). Statements about the direction of electric flux in Weeks 7, 13 and 14 use the Kogut–Susskind sign. Strong-coupling string tension $\sigma=g^2/2$.
  - $SU(N)$: $H=\frac{g^2}2\sum_\ell E^a_\ell E^a_\ell-\frac1{g^2}\sum_P\big(\operatorname{tr}U_P+\operatorname{tr}U_P^\dagger\big)$, with $E^aE^a$ the quadratic Casimir of the link representation; strong-coupling tension $\sigma=\frac{g^2}2C_2(F)$, $C_2(F)=\frac{N^2-1}{2N}$. Gauge transformations act as $U_\ell\to\Lambda_x^\dagger U_\ell\Lambda_{x+\hat\mu}$ for $\ell=(x\to x+\hat\mu)$; the right and left generators obey $[E_R^a,U]=UT^a$ and $[E_L^a,U]=-T^aU$, and the Gauss generator is $G^a_x=\sum_{\ell\ \rm in}E^a_R+\sum_{\ell\ \rm out}E^a_L$. These reduce to the $U(1)$ conventions above.
  - $\mathbb{Z}_2$ in $2+1$ dimensions: $H=-\Gamma\sum_\ell\sigma^x_\ell-K\sum_P B_P$, $B_P=\prod_{\ell\in\partial P}\sigma^z_\ell$. Here $\sigma^z_\ell=U_\ell$ is the link variable, which flips the electric flux, and $\sigma^x_\ell=(-1)^{E_\ell}$ is the electric field; Gauss law $\prod_{\ell\ni v}\sigma^x_\ell=(-1)^{q_v}$.
- **ℤ₂ gauge–Higgs model (Week 13).** $S=-\beta\sum_P\sigma_P-\kappa\sum_{\ell=\ell_\mu(x)}s_x\sigma_\ell s_{x+\hat\mu}$ with $s\in C^0$, $\sigma\in C^1$; unitary gauge $Z=2^{N_s}\sum_\sigma\exp\big(\beta\sum_P\sigma_P+\kappa\sum_\ell\sigma_\ell\big)$; abbreviations $t=\tanh\beta$, $u=\tanh\kappa$, $y=e^{-2\beta}$, $v=e^{-2\kappa}$. At strong coupling the string tension is $\sigma=\ln\coth\beta$ and the rest energy of a probe bound to a dynamical charge is $E_M=\ln\coth\kappa$, so the string breaks at $R_*=2E_M/\sigma$ (Week 13 §5). In $d=3$ the model is self-dual under $(\beta,\kappa)\to(\kappa^*,\beta^*)$ with $x^*=-\tfrac12\ln\tanh x$: $Z(\beta,\kappa)=(\sinh2\beta\,\sinh2\kappa)^{3N_s/2}Z(\kappa^*,\beta^*)$ on the $L^3$ torus, with self-dual line $\sinh2\beta\,\sinh2\kappa=1$. Hamiltonian form with matter: Gauss law $\tau^x_v\prod_{\ell\ni v}\sigma^x_\ell=1$; its unitary form is the toric code in fields, $-h\sum A_v-K\sum B_P-\Gamma\sum X-J\sum Z$, with the $e$–$m$ duality $(h,K,\Gamma,J)\to(K,h,J,\Gamma)$.
- **$\mathbb{Z}_N$ gauge theory with charge-$q$ matter (Week 14).** $a\in C^1(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z}_N)$, $\phi\in C^0(\Lambda,H)$ with $H=\langle q\rangle=r\mathbb{Z}_N$, $r=\gcd(N,q)$, and
$$
S[a,\phi]=-\beta\sum_P\cos\frac{2\pi(da)_P}{N}-\kappa\sum_\ell\cos\frac{2\pi(d\phi-qa)_\ell}{N},
$$
  invariant under $a\to a+d\lambda$, $\phi\to\phi+q\lambda$; for $N=2$, $q=1$ it is Wegner's action plus the hopping term $-\kappa\sum s_x\sigma_\ell s_y$ of Week 13. The $\mathbb{Z}_N$ character coefficients of the plaquette weight are $\tilde c_k=a_k/a_0$ with $a_k=\frac1N\sum_je^{\beta\cos(2\pi j/N)}\omega^{-kj}$: $\tilde c_1=\tanh\beta$ for $N=2$; $\tilde c_1=\tanh(\beta/2)$ and $\tilde c_2=\tanh^2(\beta/2)$ for $N=4$; $\tilde c_1=\tilde c_2=(e^{3\beta/2}-1)/(e^{3\beta/2}+2)$ for $N=3$. For comparison, the Villain $U(1)$ weight has $\tilde c_q=e^{-q^2/2\beta}$. In the Hamiltonian limit of the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ transfer matrix, $e^{-\beta_t(1-\cos2\pi/N)}=a_t\Gamma/2$ for $N\ge3$ (Week 7 §7.1 gives the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ case). The matter weight expands as $e^{\kappa\cos(2\pi qa_\ell/N)}=b_0\big[1+\sum_{j=1}^{N'-1}h_j\,\omega^{jqa_\ell}\big]$ with $N'=N/r$ and $h_j=\tilde c_j^{(N')}(\kappa)$, the coefficients of the $\mathbb{Z}_{N'}$ weight: $h_1=\tanh\kappa$ for $N'=2$ and $h_1=\kappa/2+O(\kappa^2)$ for $N'\ge3$ (Week 14 §4.3). Finite temperature: $N_\tau$ time slices, $T=1/N_\tau$, couplings $\beta_t$, $\beta_s$ on time-like and space-like plaquettes, and the Polyakov loop $L_e(\vec x)=\omega^{e\sum_\tau a_0(\vec x,\tau)}$ ($P$ stays reserved for plaquettes). Hamiltonian form: $H=-\Gamma\sum_\ell{\rm Re}\,X_\ell-K\sum_P{\rm Re}\,B_P$ with clock and shift on each link, $Z_\ell|k\rangle=\omega^k|k\rangle$ ($Z_\ell=U_\ell$, the link variable), $X_\ell|k\rangle=|k+1\rangle$ and $Z_\ell X_\ell=\omega X_\ell Z_\ell$, $G_x=\prod_iX_{(x-\hat i,i)}X^\dagger_{(x,i)}$ and physical states $G_x=1$ (with matter, $G_x\Pi_x=1$); electric-flux operators $U_i(n)=\prod_{x:\,x_i=n}X_{(x,i)}$. The charge-2 $U(1)$ model of Hansson–Oganesyan–Sondhi in the London limit is $S=-\beta\sum_P\cos(da)_P-\kappa\sum_\ell\cos(d\phi-2a)_\ell$.
- **Villain abelian-Higgs model of charge $q$ (Sem II Week 3 §5).** $S=\frac\beta2\|da-2\pi n\|^2+\frac\kappa2\|d\phi-qa-2\pi\ell\|^2$ with $a\in C^1$, $\phi\in C^0$ over one period, $n\in C^2(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})$, $\ell\in C^1(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})$; invariant under $(a,n,\ell)\to(a+2\pi k,n+dk,\ell-qk)$, $(\phi,\ell)\to(\phi+2\pi j,\ell+dj)$ and $(a,\phi)\to(a+d\lambda,\phi+q\lambda)$. With $F=da-2\pi n$ and $D\phi=d\phi-qa-2\pi\ell$, $d(D\phi)=-qF-2\pi v$ with the vortex number $v=qn+d\ell\in C^2(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})$; the photon mass is $m_A^2=q^2\kappa/\beta$, and $\kappa=\infty$ is the $\mathbb{Z}_q$ Villain theory of Sem II Week 2 (6.1).
- **Spin chains (Sem II Week 5).** $S^a=\sigma^a/2$ and $n_j=S^z_j+\frac12$ on sites $j=1,\dots,L$; translation $TS^a_jT^{-1}=S^a_{j+1}$ and momentum $T|\psi\rangle=e^{iP}|\psi\rangle$. The flux-threaded XXZ ring is $H(\varphi)=\sum_j\big[\frac J2\big(e^{i\varphi/L}S^+_{j+1}S^-_j+{\rm h.c.}\big)+J\Delta S^z_jS^z_{j+1}\big]$, with the large gauge transformation $U=\exp\big(\frac{2\pi i}L\sum_jj\,n_j\big)$. The cluster chain is $H=-\sum_jZ_{j-1}X_jZ_{j+1}$, with the edge operators of Sem II Week 5 (5.5) and the twist rule $Z_{j+L}\equiv UZ_jU^{-1}$.
- **Dualities of the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ models.**
  - 2d Ising, Kramers–Wannier: $\sinh2K\,\sinh2K^*=1$, self-dual point $K_c=\tfrac12\ln(1+\sqrt2)=0.440687$.
  - 3d $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge theory ↔ 3d Ising on $\Lambda^*$: $\tanh\beta=e^{-2K^*}$. Strong coupling (small β, confined) maps to low temperature (large $K^*$, **ordered** Ising); weak coupling (deconfined) maps to the **disordered** Ising phase. $\beta_c=0.76141$ ↔ $K^*_c=0.221654$. The dual Ising spin is the endpoint of a string of flipped plaquettes (a vison), a local operator, and it condenses in the confined phase. Prefactors: on an open box with $+$ boundary spins, $Z_g(\beta)=2^{N_\ell}(2\sinh2K^*)^{-N_P/2}Z_I^{+}(K^*)$; on the 3-torus the eight boundary-condition sectors are summed, $Z_g(\beta)=2^{N_\ell-1}(2\sinh2K^*)^{-N_P/2}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_2^3}Z_I^{(\varepsilon)}(K^*)$ (Week 5 §7.1 and F6). The value of $K^*_c$ is from Ferrenberg–Xu–Landau (2018).
  - In the Hamiltonian picture, $\sigma^x_\ell=\mu^z_P\mu^z_{P'}$ and $B_P=\mu^x_P$ map the $2+1$d $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge theory to the transverse-field Ising model $-\Gamma\sum\mu^z\mu^z-K\sum\mu^x$ on dual sites; $\Gamma\gg K$ (confined) is the ferromagnet.
  - 4d $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge theory is self-dual, $\sinh2\beta\,\sinh2\beta^*=1$, with the self-dual point at $0.440687$.
  - Kramers–Wannier operator on the periodic chain of $N$ sites (Week 4 §4.4): $H(g)=-\sum_i(\sigma^z_i\sigma^z_{i+1}+g\,\sigma^x_i)$ and $\eta=\prod_i\sigma^x_i$. The relations $D\sigma^x_i=\sigma^z_i\sigma^z_{i+1}D$ and $D\sigma^z_i\sigma^z_{i+1}=\sigma^x_{i+1}D$ fix $D$ up to a scale, chosen real; with the translation $T\sigma_iT^{-1}=\sigma_{i+1}$ they give $D^\dagger D=1+\eta$, $D^2=(1+\eta)\,T$ (with $T$, not $T^{-1}$), $D^\dagger=T^{-1}D$ and $D\,H(g)=g\,H(1/g)\,D$. The closed form (3.12) of Sem II Week 4, $D=2^{-L/2}\sum_{s,s'}(-1)^{\sum_is_i(s'_i+s'_{i+1})}|s'\rangle\langle s|$ on a chain of $L$ sites, fixes the overall sign. Transfer matrix of the classical Ising model (Sem II Week 13 §2): $V_1(K_v)=\prod_i\big(e^{K_v}+e^{-K_v}\sigma^x_i\big)=(2\sinh2K_v)^{L/2}\,e^{K_v^*\sum_i\sigma^x_i},\qquad V_2(K_h)=e^{K_h\sum_i\sigma^z_i\sigma^z_{i+1}}$, with $\tanh K^*=e^{-2K}$ and the symmetric row transfer matrix $\mathbb T=V_2^{1/2}V_1V_2^{1/2}$, the only form with which the dressed duality line $V_2^{1/2}DV_2^{1/2}$ commutes ($Z={\rm Tr}\,\mathbb T^M$ is unchanged; the letter $T$ stays the translation). A duality line on the ring is $H_D^{(d)}$ of Week 13 (3.6), moved by the two-site unitary $U_d=H\cdot CZ$; gauging on an interval uses Dirichlet ends, and the end qubits are written $\tilde\sigma$ (τ is reserved). For $\mathbb{Z}_N$ the duality operator carries the phase $\omega^{\sum_is_i(s'_i-s'_{i+1})}$. Gauging the chain puts link qubits $X,Z$ at $i+\frac12$ with Gauss law $G_i=X_{i-\frac12}\sigma^x_iX_{i+\frac12}$ ($G_i=X^\dagger_{i-\frac12}X_iX_{i+\frac12}$ for $\mathbb{Z}_N$), and the dual chain sits at the shifted sites $i+\frac12\mapsto i+1$.
- **Modified Villain** (Sem II Wk 12): promote $n$ to a $\mathbb{Z}$ gauge field with its own constraint, $dn=0$, enforced by a compact Lagrange multiplier on the dual lattice with the weight $e^{+i\langle\tilde\theta,\star dn\rangle}$ (XY) or $e^{+i\langle\tilde\phi,\star dn\rangle}$ (gauge); this sign makes the multiplier the dual field of §5, so that defects would enter as $e^{-i\langle v,\tilde\theta\rangle}$, and it is opposite to GLSS's default. The defects are removed instead of summed, and the dualities become exact: $Z_p[\Lambda;\beta]=(2\pi\beta)^{-N_{p+1}/2}Z_{d-p-2}[\Lambda^*;\tilde\beta]$ with $\tilde\beta=1/4\pi^2\beta$, where $N_{p+1}$ is the number of $(p+1)$-cells (Sem II Week 12 (2.7), (3.4)–(3.5)); the dual integer is $\tilde n=\star m$ in $d=2$ and $\tilde n=-\star m$, $a^\vee=-a$ in $d=4$. Summation by parts on the two lattices: $\langle\tilde\phi,\star d\omega\rangle=(-1)^{q+1}\langle\tilde d\tilde\phi,\star\omega\rangle$ (Week 12 (2.3)). The exact magnetic operator of monopole-free compact QED is $U^{\rm m}_\alpha(\Sigma)=e^{\frac{i\alpha}{2\pi}\sum_\Sigma F}=e^{-i\alpha\sum_\Sigma n}$, the 4d 't Hooft line is $T_m=e^{-im\sum\tilde a}$ (against $e^{+iq\sigma}$ for the 3d monopole), and a $\mathbb{Z}_N$ electric background enters as $da-2\pi n-\frac{2\pi}NB$ with the constraint $d(Nn+B)=0$.

## 5. Compact scalars and compact QED: normalizations of the dual descriptions

- **XY and BKT (Weeks 1–4).** Spin-wave exponent $\eta=1/(2\pi\beta)$ and amplitude $C(x)=e^{-\kappa/\beta}|x|^{-\eta}$ at the Gaussian level. Vortex pair energy $2\pi\beta\ln(r/a_{\rm lat})+2E_{\rm core}$ with $E_{\rm core}=2\pi^2\beta\kappa$; fugacity $y=e^{-2\pi^2\beta\kappa}$, a long-distance matching convention with vertex operators normal-ordered at the lattice scale, not an exact microscopic identity. Sine-Gordon dual: $S=\frac1{8\pi^2\beta}\int(\partial\chi)^2-2y\int\cos\chi$, with scaling dimensions $\pi\beta$ for $e^{i\chi}$ and $q^2/4\pi\beta$ for $e^{iq\theta}$. The universal statement is $\pi\beta_R=2$ at the transition for the **renormalized** stiffness; the **bare** critical couplings are model-dependent, $\beta_c\approx1.12$ for the cosine model (Hasenbusch 2005) and $\beta_c\approx0.75$ for the Villain model (Janke–Nather 1993), both above $2/\pi$ because screening lowers the stiffness.
- **Continuum compact QED.** $S=\frac1{2e^2}\int F\wedge\star F$; the unit electric charge defines the Wilson line $e^{i\oint a}$, and Dirac quantization reads $\oint_{\Sigma_2}F\in2\pi\mathbb{Z}$.
- **Dual photon in $d=3$ (erratum E2).** $\sigma\sim\sigma+2\pi$, with
$$
S[\sigma]=\frac{e^2}{8\pi^2}\int d^3x\,(\partial\sigma)^2,\qquad B_\mu\equiv\tfrac12\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho}F_{\nu\rho}=+\frac{ie^2}{2\pi}\partial_\mu\sigma\quad(\text{Euclidean}),
$$
  derived by adding $+\frac{i}{2\pi}\int\sigma\,(\partial\cdot B-2\pi\rho_m)$ to $\frac1{2e^2}\int B^2$ and integrating out $B$; the $2\pi$-periodicity of σ is Dirac quantization. With this sign a monopole of magnetic charge $q$ ($\oint B=2\pi q$) is the insertion $e^{iq\sigma}$, and $\langle B_\mu(x)\,e^{iq\sigma(0)}\rangle/\langle e^{iq\sigma(0)}\rangle=q\,x_\mu/2|x|^3$ carries outward flux $2\pi q$. (The opposite pairing, $B=-\frac{ie^2}{2\pi}\partial\sigma$ with $e^{-iq\sigma}$, is the same convention after $\sigma\to-\sigma$; sources that use it are translated before a formula is imported.) Lattice monopoles, whose flux is $-2\pi m_c$ (§4), enter as $e^{-im_c\sigma}$, which is immaterial once $\pm$ charges are summed. The magnetic current is $j^\mu_{\rm mag}=\frac1{4\pi}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho}F_{\nu\rho}=B_\mu/2\pi$.
- **Monopole gas in $d=3$.** Pair interaction $\frac{4\pi^2}{e^2}\sum_{i<j}q_iq_jG_3(x_i-x_j)$, equivalently $\frac{2\pi^2}{e^2}\sum_{i\ne j}$. Villain monopole action $S_{\rm mono}=2\pi^2\beta\,G_3(0)=4.99\,\beta$ with $\beta=1/(e^2a_{\rm lat})$; fugacity $\zeta\propto e^{-S_{\rm mono}}$ per unit volume. Dilute-gas effective action $S=\int\big[\frac{e^2}{8\pi^2}(\partial\sigma)^2-2\zeta\cos\sigma\big]$, photon mass $m_\gamma^2=8\pi^2\zeta/e^2$, equal to the Debye mass $m_D$ of the gas. The small parameter that controls the dilute-gas and semiclassical steps is $\zeta/e^6$. Vertex operators are normal-ordered at the lattice scale, $V_\pm=e^{S_{\rm mono}}e^{\pm i\sigma}$; in the monopole-free theory $|\langle e^{i\sigma}\rangle|=e^{-S_{\rm mono}}$. Plasma scales (Week 9 §2.5): mean separation $\bar r=(2\zeta)^{-1/3}$, Bjerrum length $\ell_B=\pi/e^2$, Debye length $\lambda_D=1/m_D$, with $\lambda_D/\bar r=2^{5/6}e/(4\pi\zeta^{1/6})$ and $\ell_B/\lambda_D=2\sqrt2\pi^2(\zeta/e^6)^{1/2}$; on the Villain lattice the ordered regime holds for $\beta\gtrsim4$–5.
- **The Wilson loop in compact QED₃ (Week 10).** $K\equiv e^2/8\pi^2$ is the dual-photon stiffness, $S=\int\big[K(\partial\sigma)^2-2\zeta\cos\sigma\big]$, so $m_\gamma^2=\zeta/K$ (the letter $A$ stays reserved for the area of a loop). Through the moves of Week 8 a charge-$q$ Wilson loop on $C$ becomes $d\tilde n=-q\star J_C$: σ winds by $2\pi q$ around $C$ in the right-handed sense. Each monopole sees the loop through $\eta_C=2\pi\omega_\Sigma=\Omega_C/2$, with the solid angle $\Omega_C$ positive on the side of the right-handed normal of the spanning surface, and in the plasma $\cos\sigma\to\cos(\sigma-q\eta_C)$. Pinned values: the kink $\sigma_{\rm k}=4\arctan e^{m_\gamma\xi}$; the string tension $\sigma_{\rm str}=16Km_\gamma=2e^2m_\gamma/\pi^2$; the number of monopoles per Debye volume $N_D=2\zeta/m_\gamma^3=2K/m_\gamma$, whose inverse sets the size of loop corrections; the deconfinement temperature $T_c=2e^2/\pi$, with $\beta_{XY}=T/e^2$ for the dual-photon XY model of the thermal theory.
- **Duality in $d=4$.** Villain $U(1)$ at coupling $\beta$ is dual to Villain $U(1)$ on $\Lambda^*$ at $\tilde\beta=1/(4\pi^2\beta)$, i.e. $\tilde e=2\pi/e$; the dual field has no dual monopoles and couples to the integer monopole current, which is conserved.
- **Dual variables on the lattice (Week 8).** In the gauge weeks the Hubbard–Stratonovich integer, the electric flux, is $b\in C^2(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})$ with $\delta b=0$; $m=dn$ is reserved for the monopole number (in the XY weeks the current integer is $m\in C^1(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})$ and the vorticity is $v=dn$). With $\tilde\varphi$ and $\tilde A$ the Poisson conjugates of the integer dual fields in the form of §3, the compact dual fields are $\sigma=-2\pi\tilde\varphi$ ($d=3$) and $\tilde a=-2\pi\tilde A$ ($d=4$); then $F\simeq\frac{i}{2\pi\beta}\star d\sigma$, i.e. $B=+\frac{ie^2}{2\pi}\partial\sigma$, and $F\simeq2\pi i\tilde\beta\star d\tilde a$, and the monopoles couple as $e^{-im_c\sigma}$ and $e^{-i\langle j,\tilde a\rangle}$. The fugacity in the lattice normal-ordering scheme is $\zeta=e^{-S_{\rm mono}}/a_{\rm lat}^3$.
- **Monopole current in $d=4$.** $j=\star dn$ on dual links, $j_4=+m_{123}$, $j_3=-m_{124}$, $j_2=+m_{134}$, $j_1=-m_{234}$ (the shuffle signs of §2), and $\delta j=0$ follows from $\star\star=-1$ on $C^3$ in $d=4$.
- **Four-dimensional compact QED (Week 11).** Villain–Wilson matching by the first character, $e^{-1/2\beta_V}=I_1(\beta_W)/I_0(\beta_W)$ (good to a few per cent). The twisted-sheet operator $T_\alpha$, the plaquette weights shifted by α on the plaquettes dual to a closed dual surface, is the generator of the electric $U(1)^{(1)}$; in the Villain theory with dynamical monopoles the integer 't Hooft loop is a relabeling of the Villain integers and equals 1 identically, since dynamical monopoles screen it. Dual Ginzburg–Landau normalization: $\frac1{4\tilde e^2}\tilde F^2+|\tilde D\phi|^2+\frac\lambda4(|\phi|^2-v^2)^2$, with $m_V^2=2\tilde e^2v^2$ and $m_H^2=\lambda v^2$. Julia–Toulouse/Kalb–Ramond form of the monopole condensate: $\frac1{2e^2}(da-\Lambda)^2+\frac1{2g^2}(d\Lambda)^2$, with mass $m^2=g^2/e^2$, dual to the BF form $-\frac{i}{2\pi}\int\Lambda\wedge d\tilde a$ with the dual photon. Continuum inner product $\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=\int\alpha\wedge\star\beta$. Further lattice values: $G_4(\hat e)=G_4(0)-\tfrac18$, and the Bessel representation $G_d(x)=\int_0^\infty dt\,e^{-2dt}\prod_\mu I_{x_\mu}(2t)$.
- **Magnetic current in $d=4$ (Sem II Week 3 §3).** $J_m^{\nu\mu}=\frac1{4\pi}\epsilon_{\nu\mu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}$ with $\epsilon_{\tau123}=+1$; a 't Hooft line of charge $m$ on $C$ is defined by $\partial_\mu J_m^{\nu\mu}=m\,J_C^\nu$, outward magnetic flux $2\pi m$.
- **Transport of sources.** For $\eta\in C^3(\Lambda)$, $\star(\delta\eta)=-d(\star\eta)$ in both $d=3$ and $d=4$, a case of $\delta=(-1)^{d(p+1)+1}\star d\star$.

## 6. Extended operators, symmetry operators and the form-degree master table

- Wilson operator of charge $q$ on a closed $p$-cycle $C$: $W_q(C)=\exp(iq\sum_Ca)$, or $\omega^{q\sum_Ca}$ in the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ case.
- 't Hooft and disorder operators live on cycles of $\Lambda^*$ and are defined by their modification of the path-integral sum (Kadanoff–Ceva style in Sem I Wk 4; the 4d 't Hooft loop, which flips the plaquettes dual to a dual surface bounded by $\tilde C$, in Wk 11).
- **Symmetry and 't Hooft operators of $\mathbb{Z}_N$ lattice gauge theory.** Replace $(da)_P$ by $(da)_P-1$ in the plaquette weights (reverse β for $\mathbb{Z}_2$) on the plaquettes dual to a $(d-2)$-chain $\tilde c$ of $\Lambda^*$. If $\tilde c$ is closed and bounds, $\tilde c=\partial\tilde V$, let $\lambda_{\tilde V}$ be the signed indicator of the links dual to the cells of $\tilde V$, oriented so that $d\lambda_{\tilde V}=+1$ on the plaquettes dual to $\tilde c$; the change of variables $a\to a+\lambda_{\tilde V}$ removes the insertion, and the result is the generator $U(\tilde c)$ of the electric 1-form symmetry, with $\langle U\rangle=1$ in every phase and $\langle W(C)\,U(\tilde c)\rangle=\omega^{{\rm Link}(C,\tilde c)}\langle W(C)\rangle$. This **defines** the linking number of a loop of $\Lambda$ and a cycle of $\Lambda^*$: ${\rm Link}(C,\partial\tilde V)\equiv\langle J_C,\lambda_{\tilde V}\rangle=\#(C\cap\tilde V)$, the signed number of links of $C$ dual to cells of $\tilde V$ (Weeks 5, 11 and 14). For $U(1)$ the twisted sheet replaces $da-2\pi n$ by $da-2\pi n-\alpha\,d\lambda_{\tilde V}$ and gives $e^{iq\alpha\,{\rm Link}(C,\tilde c)}$ on $W_q(C)$ (Week 11 §5.1). On a closed $\tilde c$ that does not bound, such as a noncontractible cycle of a torus, the same insertion is a twisted boundary condition, and $\langle U\rangle=Z_{\rm tw}/Z$ need not equal 1 (Week 5 F6; Week 15 §3.4). If $\tilde c$ has a boundary, the result is a 't Hooft (disorder) operator of dimension $d-3$ supported on $\partial\tilde c$: the vison, a local operator, in $d=3$, and the 't Hooft loop $\tilde W$ in $d=4$ (Week 5 §8).
- A $q$-form symmetry acts on $q$-dimensional charged objects. Its symmetry operator is $U_\alpha(\Sigma)=\exp\big(i\alpha\oint_\Sigma\omega\big)$ on a closed $(d-q-1)$-dimensional $\Sigma$, with $\omega$ the closed form listed below; the charge is measured by linking, $U_\alpha(\Sigma)W(C)=e^{i\alpha\,{\rm Link}(\Sigma,C)}W(C)U_\alpha(\Sigma)$ as a statement about correlators. On a time slice the operator statement is $U_\alpha W_q(\Gamma)U_\alpha^{-1}=e^{-iq\alpha I_s}W_q(\Gamma)$, with $I_s$ the intersection number of the closed surface and the line in the slice; the minus sign is the jump $-I_s$ of the spacetime linking number as the slice passes the line. In the continuum, Link $=+1$ for a worldline running in $+\tau$ and the outward-oriented sphere around it ($d=4$), and for two loops by the right-hand rule ($d=3$). The closed forms listed below are the real-time ones; in Euclidean signature they carry a factor of $i$, $\omega_E=i\beta\star d\theta$ for the shift symmetry of the compact scalar and $-\frac{i}{e^2}\star F$ for the electric symmetry of Maxwell theory (Sem II Week 1 §§3, 6).
- Ground-state degeneracy bookkeeping on $T^d$ and $\Sigma_g$ uses $H_1$ with the intersection pairing; clock–shift pairs $(Z,X)$ obey $ZX=\omega XZ$.

- **Lattice versus TQFT.** The $\mathbb{Z}_N$ BF row describes the deconfined topological limit. The Wegner lattice model has only the electric $\mathbb{Z}_2^{(1)}$ as an exact symmetry; its magnetic symmetry emerges in the deconfined phase, since visons are dynamical at finite β.
- **Low dimensions.** For Maxwell theory in $d=3$ the magnetic symmetry is a **0-form** $U(1)$ whose charged operators are the **local** monopole operators $e^{i\sigma}$, and the dual photon is the Goldstone boson of its spontaneous breaking in the monopole-free theory. In $d=4$ both symmetries are 1-form and act on Wilson and 't Hooft **lines**.
- **Projective representations and anomalies (Sem II Week 5).** $U(g)U(h)=\omega(g,h)\,U(gh)$; a rephasing $U\to\beta U$ changes ω by the coboundary $\delta\beta(g,h)=\beta(g)\beta(h)/\beta(gh)$; for abelian $G$ the class is detected by $c(g,h)=\omega(g,h)/\omega(h,g)$. For $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$ the Pauli representation $U(a^{g_1}b^{g_2})=X^{g_1}Z^{g_2}$ has $\omega(g,h)=(-1)^{g_2h_1}$; for $\mathbb{Z}_N\times\mathbb{Z}_N$ class $k$ is $U(a)=X$, $U(b)=Z^{-k}$, $c(a,b)=e^{2\pi ik/N}$ (with $ZX=\omega XZ$, $XZ^k=\omega^{-k}Z^kX$). An 't Hooft anomaly is a phase $Z[A^\lambda]=e^{i\mathcal A(A,\lambda)}Z[A]$ under background gauge transformations that no local counterterm removes, $\mathcal A\to\mathcal A+S_{\rm ct}[A^\lambda]-S_{\rm ct}[A]$. The $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$ SPT weight is $(-1)^{\sum A\cup B}$; its edge obeys $Z_{\rm edge}[A,B+d\beta]=(-1)^{\sum_tA_t\beta_{t+1}}Z_{\rm edge}[A,B]$ with transfer matrices $M_t=e^{-\epsilon H}V(a)^{A_t}V(b)^{B_t}$, later times to the left.
- **Group cohomology, MPS and anyon data (Sem II Week 7).** Inhomogeneous $U(1)$-valued cochains of $G$ with coboundary
$$
(\delta\omega)(g_1,\dots,g_{n+1})=\omega(g_2,\dots,g_{n+1})\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}\omega(g_1,\dots,g_ig_{i+1},\dots,g_{n+1})^{(-1)^i}\;\omega(g_1,\dots,g_n)^{(-1)^{n+1}},
$$
  normalized ($\omega=1$ whenever an argument is the identity); in degree 2 this is the coboundary of the projective-representation bullet above. Homogeneous form: $\nu(g_0,\dots,g_d)=\omega\big(g_0^{-1}g_1,\ \dots,\ g_{d-1}^{-1}g_d\big),\qquad\nu(gg_0,\dots,gg_d)=\nu(g_0,\dots,g_d)$, with simplex weights $\nu^{s_\Delta}$. Representatives: $(-1)^{xyz}$ for $H^3(\mathbb{Z}_2,U(1))=\mathbb{Z}_2$ and $\omega_k(x,y,z)=\exp\big(\frac{2\pi ik}{N}\,x\,{\rm carry}(y,z)\big)$ for $\mathbb{Z}_N$. Matrix-product states: $E(X)=\sum_sA^sXA^{s\dagger},\qquad E^*(Y)=\sum_sA^{s\dagger}YA^s,\qquad E_O(X)=\sum_{s,s'}O_{ss'}\,A^{s'}XA^{s\dagger}$, normalized by $E(\mathbb 1)=\mathbb 1$ and $E^*(\rho)=\rho>0$, ${\rm tr}\rho=1$; a symmetry acts as $(u\cdot A)^s=\sum_{s'}u_{ss'}A^{s'}$ and on the virtual space as $u\cdot A=e^{i\theta}V^\dagger AV$. Anyons: $F$ maps $(X\otimes Y)\otimes Z\to X\otimes(Y\otimes Z)$, $R$ maps $X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X$, $\theta_X=R(X,X)$; for Dijkgraaf–Witten theories $F=\omega$ on fluxes and $R(X,Y)=\chi_X(y)$. The lattice $\mathbb{Z}_2$ theory of class $k$ (the letter $k$ is also the Chern–Simons level of §10; context separates them) is
$$
Z_k(M)=\frac1{2^{N_0}}\sum_{a\in Z^1(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z}_2)}(-1)^{k\sum_{\rm cubes}a\cup a\cup a}=\frac1{|H^0|}\sum_{[a]\in H^1(M,\mathbb{Z}_2)}(-1)^{k\langle a^3,[M]\rangle},
$$
  and the double semion has labels $\{1,b,s,\bar s\}$ with $\theta_s=+i$.
  Six-index $F$-symbols and the pentagon are Sem II Week 11 (2.1)–(2.2); for abelian data they reduce to $F(a,b,c)$, and $[F^{aaa}_a]_{00}=\kappa_a/d_a$, so that a closed loop of label $a$ evaluates to $\kappa_ad_a$, which is not a quantum dimension when $\kappa_a=-1$; the label 0 is the null string and 1 the trivial anyon.
- **Gauging.** Gauging a $\mathbb{Z}_N^{(q)}$ symmetry in $d$ dimensions produces a dual $\mathbb{Z}_N^{(d-q-2)}$ symmetry (for $q=0$, $d=2$: Kramers–Wannier). The degree $d-p-1$ of the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ magnetic symmetry and the degree $d-q-2$ of the dual symmetry are different statements. With the normalization of Sem II Week 4 (2.4)–(2.5), $Z_{T/\mathbb{Z}_N^{(p)}}=\frac{|H^{p-1}||H^{p-3}|\cdots}{|H^p||H^{p-2}|\cdots}\sum_{[B]\in H^{p+1}}Z_T[B]$ (prefactor $1/|H^0|$ for $p=0$ and $|H^0|/|H^1|$ for $p=1$); the dual background $C\in C^{d-p-1}$ couples as $\omega^{\sum B\cup C}$, and gauging the dual symmetry with $\omega^{-\sum B'\cup C}$ returns $T$ up to $N^{\pm\chi(X)}$, which is 1 on tori. Aharony–Seiberg–Tachikawa label lines by $(z_e,z_m)\in\mathbb{Z}_N\times\mathbb{Z}_N$ with mutual locality $z_ez'_m-z_mz'_e\equiv0$ mod $N$, and θ → θ + 2π acts as $(z_e,z_m)\to(z_e+z_m,z_m)$, the direction of §10.
- **Higher-form Coleman–Mermin–Wagner.** A continuous $q$-form symmetry cannot be spontaneously broken if $q\ge d-2$ (GKSW §5). So a continuous 1-form symmetry can break only for $d\ge4$; $d=3$ is the marginal case, where the charged loop has a logarithmic potential. Discrete symmetries break in lower dimensions: $\mathbb{Z}_2^{(0)}$ in $d=2$ (Ising), $\mathbb{Z}_2^{(1)}$ in $d=3$ (the deconfined $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge theory); a discrete $q$-form symmetry cannot break if $q\ge d-1$ (Sem II Week 3 §4).
- **SSB criterion for 1-form symmetries.** A perimeter law for the charged loop means the symmetry is spontaneously broken; an area law means it is unbroken. Precisely (Sem II Week 3 (2.1)): dilate a fixed shape $C$ by λ; the symmetry is unbroken if $|\langle W(C_\lambda)\rangle|\,e^{\int_{C_\lambda}\mu\,ds}\to0$ for every local counterterm μ, and broken if it stays bounded away from zero for some μ. The product need not converge: corners add powers of λ that no local term removes. The criterion presupposes an exact symmetry: charged dynamical matter breaks the electric 1-form symmetry explicitly, and then Wilson loops obey a perimeter law in every phase (string breaking).
- **Realizations in Semester I.** (In the Villain lattice theory, where monopoles are dynamical, the integer 't Hooft loop is screened and equals 1 identically; statements about 't Hooft loops below refer to their perimeter behaviour or to the continuum limit.) 3d compact QED: electric $U(1)^{(1)}$ unbroken (area law at every coupling), magnetic $U(1)^{(0)}$ explicitly broken by monopoles, photon gapped. 4d compact QED, Coulomb phase: both loops obey perimeter laws; the electric 1-form symmetry and the emergent magnetic one are spontaneously broken. 4d confining phase: Wilson loops obey an area law and 't Hooft loops a perimeter law; the electric symmetry is unbroken and the magnetic one explicitly broken. Gauge–Higgs with fundamental matter: the electric symmetry is explicitly broken, so no 1-form symmetry distinguishes Higgs from confinement (Fradkin–Shenker). $\mathbb{Z}_N$ gauge theory with charge-$q$ matter keeps the residual $\mathbb{Z}_{\gcd(N,q)}^{(1)}$.

## 7. $\mathbb{Z}_N$ fields and BF presentation

- $\mathbb{Z}_N$ $p$-form field: $a\in C^p(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z}_N)$, represented by integers mod $N$; $\omega=e^{2\pi i/N}$.
- $\mathbb{Z}_N$ BF weight in $d=4$ for a 1-form $\mathbb{Z}_N$ gauge field: $\exp\big(\frac{2\pi i}N\sum b\cup da\big)$ with $a\in C^1(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z}_N)$ and $b\in C^2(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z}_N)$ both on $\Lambda$ and the cup product of §8; in $d=3$ the same with $b\in C^1$. The GLSS presentation with $b$ on $\Lambda^*$ is equivalent; Sem II Week 2 §6 gives the dictionary. With $\Lambda^*=\Lambda+h$, $h=\frac12(1,\dots,1)$, and $\tilde b(y+h)$ the dual-lattice copy of $b$, $\sum b\cup da=\langle\tilde b,\star da\rangle_{\Lambda^*}=\langle a,\star d\tilde b\rangle_\Lambda$: the cup product places $b$ on $\Lambda+h$ and $a\cup db$ on $\Lambda-h$, a lattice framing of the two kinds of line. GLSS's integer is $\tilde n=-\tilde b$ up to their orientation convention (their weight is $e^{-S}$). Normalized, $Z=N^{-N_0-N_1}\sum\omega^{\sum b\cup da}=|H^1|/|H^0|$ in $d=3$. In the continuum, with orientation $dt\wedge dx\wedge dy$ and $\epsilon^{xy}=+1$, the bracket is $[a_i(x),b_j(y)]=\frac{2\pi i}N\epsilon_{ij}\delta^2(x-y)$; the generator of the electric $\mathbb{Z}_N^{(1)}$ is the $b$-line $U_s=V_{-s}=\exp(-is\oint b)$, i.e. $\omega_e=-\frac N{2\pi}b$, so that $\langle W_eU_s\rangle=\omega^{es\,{\rm Link}}$ with the Link of §6, and the $a$-line generates the magnetic one. In the Hamiltonian lattice form, $e^{i\int b}$ across a link is $X^\dagger$ and $U$ is a product of $X$. In the continuum the weight is $\exp\big(\frac{iN}{2\pi}\int b\wedge da\big)$.
- When comparing with the group's Julia–Toulouse manuscript, that manuscript's normalizations take precedence; discrepancies get a margin note in the lecture notes rather than a silent fix.
- **Higher-gauging walls (Sem II Week 14).** In four-dimensional $\mathbb{Z}_N$ gauge theory, $H=\langle k\rangle=r\mathbb{Z}_N\cong\mathbb{Z}_{N'}$ is a subgroup of the *electric* $\mathbb{Z}_N^{(1)}$, a group of fluxes labelling the surfaces $U_h$, $h\in H$, and the wall on a closed 3-manifold $Y$ is $D_H(Y)=\mathcal N_Y^{-1}\sum_{S\in Z_2(Y;H)}U[S]$, with the manuscript's $\mathcal N_Y$ fixed by letting a transparent line cross with amplitude 1 ($\mathcal N_Y=|Z_2(Y;H)|$ when every closed surface bounds). The slab normalization of Sem II Week 13 (4.2) gives $C_1=Z_H(Y)\,D_H$ with $Z_H(Y)=|H^1(Y;H)|/|H^0(Y;H)|$. A charge-$q$ Wilson line crosses intact iff $q\in{\rm Ann}(H)=N'\mathbb{Z}_N$; otherwise it carries the endpoint charge $\alpha={\rm res}_H(q)=q$ mod $N'\in\widehat H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{N'}$. **Two meanings of $H$:** in Sem I Week 14 §3, Sem II Week 13 §5 and Sem II Week 14 §4.1, $H$ is a group of condensed *charges*, whose Wilson lines end on the sheet, and a flux $s$ passes iff $s\in{\rm Ann}(H)$; the pairing $\omega^{qh}$ exchanges the two readings, and each note says which it uses. Wall currents $\ell_e\in\mathbb{Z}_{N'}$, $\ell_{-e}=-\ell_e$, follow the manuscript's signs: the outgoing divergence $(\partial_Y\ell)_v=\sum_{e\text{ out of }v}\ell_e=-(\partial\ell)_v$, the spin difference $\Delta\theta_e=\theta_v-\theta_{v'}=-(d\theta)_e$ for $e:v\to v'$, the source $J=\alpha(\delta_x-\delta_{x'})$, and the junction $E_\alpha$ represented by the clock operator $\omega_{N'}^{-\alpha\theta}$. The periodic-Gaussian weight is $W_\kappa(\ell)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\frac\kappa2(\ell+N'm)^2}$, $w_\kappa=W_\kappa/W_\kappa(0)$. Exchange charges of bulk monopoles entering the wall are written ν, with $r_{\rm ex}=\gcd(N',\{\nu\})$ (the manuscript's $r$ and $d$).

## 8. Cup products

- **Definition** (the cubical cup product). For $\alpha\in C^p$, $\beta\in C^q$ and a $(p+q)$-cell $(x;S)$,
$$
(\alpha\cup\beta)(x;S)=\sum_{\substack{A\subset S\\|A|=p}}\epsilon(A,S\setminus A)\;\alpha(x;A)\;\beta\big(x+\hat e_A;\,S\setminus A\big),
$$
  a sum over the shuffles of $S$ with their signs: α sits on the face at the base corner, β on the complementary face at the far corner. Chen–Tata (arXiv:2106.05274, eqs. (25)–(26)) order the factors the other way, so their product is $(-1)^{pq}$ times ours with the factors exchanged; their formulas, eq. (29) in particular, are translated before use. In low degrees: $(f\cup\beta)(\ell)=f(\text{tail})\,\beta(\ell)$, $(\beta\cup f)(\ell)=\beta(\ell)\,f(\text{head})$, $(f\cup\omega)(P(x))=f(x)\,\omega(P)$, $(\omega\cup f)(P_{\mu\nu}(x))=\omega(P)\,f(x+\hat\mu+\hat\nu)$, and for two 1-cochains the rule has **two** terms,
$$
(\alpha\cup\beta)\big(P_{\mu\nu}(x)\big)=\alpha_\mu(x)\,\beta_\nu(x+\hat\mu)-\alpha_\nu(x)\,\beta_\mu(x+\hat\nu)\qquad(\mu<\nu).
$$
- **Leibniz.** $d(\alpha\cup\beta)=d\alpha\cup\beta+(-1)^p\alpha\cup d\beta$, checked on random integer cochains for every degree pair up to $d=5$. With the single first term alone, Leibniz fails.
- **Higher cup product $\cup_1$ (Sem II Week 6 §3).** For $\alpha\in C^p$, $\beta\in C^q$ and a $(p+q-1)$-cell $(x;S)$,
$$
(\alpha\cup_1\beta)(x;S)=\sum_{i\in S}\ \sum_{A\sqcup C=S\setminus\{i\}}(-1)^{|C_>|}\,\epsilon\big(\{i\}\cup A,\,C\big)\ \alpha\big(x+\hat e_{C_>};\{i\}\cup A\big)\ \beta\big(x+\hat e_{A_<};\{i\}\cup C\big),
$$
  with the notation of the cup product above ($C_>$, $A_<$ the subsets of directions that follow or precede, ε the shuffle sign). It equals $(-1)^{pq}$ times the $\cup_1$ of Chen–Tata with the factors exchanged, and it obeys
$$
d(\alpha\cup_1\beta)=d\alpha\cup_1\beta+(-1)^p\,\alpha\cup_1d\beta+(-1)^{p+q-1}\,\alpha\cup\beta+(-1)^{pq+p+q}\,\beta\cup\alpha,
$$
  so that for cocycles $\alpha\cup\beta-(-1)^{pq}\beta\cup\alpha=(-1)^{p+q-1}d(\alpha\cup_1\beta)$ (checked in $d=3,4,5$ for every degree pair). The overall sign is fixed by Chen–Tata's recursion, their (29).
- **Pontryagin square.** For a lift $\tilde B\in C^2(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})$ of $B\in Z^2(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z}_N)$, $\mathcal P(\tilde B)=\tilde B\cup\tilde B+\tilde B\cup_1d\tilde B\ \in C^4(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z}_{2N})$ for $N$ even, and $\tilde B\cup\tilde B$ mod $N$ for $N$ odd; $\int\mathcal P(B)$ is independent of the lift and of gauge transformations. A twisted $SU(N)$ bundle on $T^4$ has fractional instanton number $Q\equiv\frac{N-1}{2N}\int\mathcal P(B)$ mod 1 ($\frac14\int\mathcal P(B)$ for $SU(2)$).
- **Graded commutativity.** On the lattice $\alpha\cup\beta\ne(-1)^{pq}\beta\cup\alpha$ in general, and the non-commutativity is physical. For **cocycles** the difference is exact, $\alpha\cup\beta-(-1)^{pq}\beta\cup\alpha=(-1)^{p+q-1}d(\alpha\cup_1\beta)$, with the $\cup_1$ defined in the bullet above (Sem II Week 6 §3); for general cochains the formula acquires the extra terms $d\alpha\cup_1\beta$ and $\alpha\cup_1d\beta$ of the coboundary formula there. Check on $T^2$: for the harmonic 1-cochains of Week 2, $\sum_Ph^x\cup h^y=L^2$ and $\sum_Ph^y\cup h^x=-L^2$, the intersection form.

## 9. Hamiltonian conventions (Semester II Block 3)

- Qubits on links; Pauli operators $X,Z$; $\mathbb{Z}_N$ generalization by clock and shift.
- Toric code: $A_v=\prod_{\ell\ni v}X_\ell$ (star), $B_p=\prod_{\ell\in\partial p}Z_\ell$ (plaquette), $H=-\sum_vA_v-\sum_pB_p$. It is the $\Gamma\to0$ point of the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonian of §4 with the magnetic term rescaled and Gauss's law imposed energetically.
- Anyons: $e$ (star violation), $m$ (plaquette violation), $\varepsilon=e\times m$; braiding phase of $e$ around $m$: $-1$ ($\omega^{qq'}$ in $\mathbb{Z}_N$).
- Entanglement conventions: von Neumann entropy $S(\rho_A)=-\operatorname{tr}\rho_A\ln\rho_A$ (natural log; the TEE of the toric code is $\gamma=\ln2$).
- **Toric code (Sem II Week 8).** On the $L_1\times L_2$ square torus, $h(x,y)$ and $v(x,y)$ are the horizontal and vertical links at $(x,y)$ and $p(x,y)$ the plaquette with lower-left corner $(x,y)$; the logical operators are $\bar Z_1=W(C_x)$, $\bar X_1=U(\tilde C_y)$, $\bar Z_2=W(C_y)$, $\bar X_2=U(\tilde C_x)$. The fermion ε is $e\times m$ with the $m$ in the plaquette north-east of the $e$ site, and $W_\varepsilon$ uses a push-off of the dual path. For $\mathbb{Z}_N$, $X_\ell=\omega^{-E_\ell}$ and $G_x=\omega^{q_x}$, and an open $Z$-string from $x$ to $y$ creates Kogut–Susskind charges $+1$ at $x$ and $-1$ at $y$ (physical charges $-1$ and $+1$, §4). Modular data: $S$ and $T$ are those of Kitaev (2006), eq. (223), with $T={\rm diag}(\theta_a)$ and no factor $e^{-2\pi ic/24}$, so that $(ST)^3=\Theta C$ with $\Theta=D^{-1}\sum_ad_a^2\theta_a$; the monodromy (full braiding) is written $M_{ab}$ in Sem II Weeks 7–8 and $B(a,b)$ in Week 10, the same object.
- **String nets (Sem II Week 11).** String basis $n_\ell=(1-X_\ell)/2$, as in Levin–Wen §II; vertex and plaquette projectors $Q_v=(1+A_v)/2$ and $B_p=(1+d_1B_p^1)/2$, so that the toric-code string net is half of the toric-code Hamiltonian plus a constant; the double-semion plaquette operator is Sem II Week 11 (4.4), with the projector $P_p$ onto closed strings; the Wilson loop is $W=\prod Z$. A string tension in the wave function is written ζ, with fugacity $t=e^{-2\zeta}$ and the auxiliary Ising coupling $\tanh K=t$ (ζ is also the monopole fugacity of Semester I, and $K$ is context-dependent).
- **Entanglement, circuits and codes (Sem II Week 9).** For a bipartition of the links, $|\partial A|$ is the number of stars the cut separates; the toric-code disk entropy is $S=|\partial A|\ln2-\ln2$, and the topological entropies are $S_{\rm topo}=-\gamma$ in the Kitaev–Preskill combination and $-2\gamma$ in the Levin–Wen one, with $\gamma=\ln\mathcal D$, $\mathcal D^2=\sum_ad_a^2$. A local-unitary circuit has depth $M$ and range $l$, with light-cone radius $R=Ml$ measured between link midpoints in the max norm. Codes are written $[\![n,k,d]\!]$ (double brackets, to avoid the wikilink syntax), with $d$ the minimal weight of a nontrivial logical operator; the planar code with its rough and smooth edges and its logical operators is Sem II Week 9 (5.6)–(5.8). Decoding: the reference chain carries $\eta=-1$ on its links, the random-bond Ising coupling is $e^{-2K}=p/(1-p)$ on the Nishimori line, and the seam is τ.
- **Condensation sheets (Sem II Week 13 §5).** For a subgroup $H$ of the 1-form symmetry of a 2+1d $\mathbb{Z}_N$ gauge theory, $S_H(\Sigma)=\frac1{|H^0(\Sigma;H)|}\sum_{\gamma\in H_1(\Sigma;H)}W(\gamma)$, with fusion $S_H\times\bar S_H=|H|^{2g-1}S_H$ on $\Sigma_g$ and lattice form $S_H=|H|^{-N_P}\sum_{c\in Z_1(\Lambda;H)}W(c)$; Roumpedakis–Seifnashri–Shao normalize by $1/\sqrt{|H_1|}$, which agrees on the torus and differs elsewhere by an Euler counterterm (the group's manuscript takes precedence where it fixes its own normalization, §7). On $T^2$ the anyon basis is $|e^am^b\rangle$.

## 10. θ-terms and the Witten effect

- $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$. **Minkowski**, with orientation $dt\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^2\wedge dx^3$: $S_\theta=\frac\theta{8\pi^2}\int F\wedge F=-\frac\theta{4\pi^2}\int d^4x\,E\cdot B$, independently of the metric signature. **Euclidean**, with $\tau=it$ and orientation $d\tau\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^2\wedge dx^3$: the θ-term contributes the phase $e^{i\theta Q}$ to the weight, i.e. $S_E\supset-i\theta Q$, with $Q=\frac1{8\pi^2}\int F\wedge F$. For $U(1)$, $Q=\frac12\int c_1\wedge c_1$ with $c_1=F/2\pi$, an integer on closed spin 4-manifolds (such as $T^4$) and in $\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}$ in general. The electric potential enters as $a=-\varphi\,dt+A\cdot dx$, so that $W_q=e^{iq\oint a}$ is the worldline of a positive charge $q$. On the 4d lattice, when a formula distinguishes Euclidean time, time is axis 1, so the orientation (1234) is $d\tau\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^2\wedge dx^3$ and $Q_{\rm lat}=\frac1{8\pi^2}\sum(F\cup F)(x;1234)$; a note that puts time on axis 4 (Week 8 §5.3 labels its current components that way) flips the sign of $Q$ and says so.
- **Witten effect.** With $\oint B=2\pi n_m$, Gauss's law $\nabla\cdot\big(E/e^2-\theta B/4\pi^2\big)=n_e\,\delta^3(x)$ gives the electric charge
$$
q_e\equiv\oint\frac{E}{e^2}\cdot dS=n_e+\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\,n_m ,
$$
  so the spectrum at $\theta+2\pi$ coincides with the spectrum at θ: the dyon labelled $(n_e,n_m)$ at $\theta+2\pi$ carries the charge of $(n_e+n_m,n_m)$ at θ. With $\tau=\frac\theta{2\pi}+\frac{2\pi i}{e^2}$, the duality group acts by $T:\theta\to\theta+2\pi$ and $S:(n_e,n_m)\to(-n_m,n_e)$; the Dirac pairing is $\langle v,w\rangle=n_en_m'-n_mn_e'$; CP at $\theta=\pi$ acts as $(n_e,n_m)\to(-n_e-n_m,n_m)$. A θ-wall carries a Chern–Simons term of level $\Delta\theta/2\pi$ (orientation $dt\wedge dx\wedge dy$) and the Hall conductivity $\sigma_{xy}=\frac{\Delta\theta}{2\pi}\frac{e^2}{2\pi}$ (Week 12 §8). Dirac pairing on the lattice: integer linking of electric and magnetic worldobjects via the cochain pairing.
- **Yang–Mills with a $\mathbb{Z}_N$ 2-form background (Sem II Week 6).** $S_W[U,B]=\frac\beta N\sum_P{\rm Re\,tr}\big(1-\omega^{-B_P}U_P\big)$ with $B\in Z^2(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z}_N)$; the continuum statements read the background as $da-B$. With the counterterm $\frac{2\pi ip}{N}\int\frac{\mathcal P(B)}2$, time reversal at θ = π maps the counterterm label as $p\to-p-(N-1)$ and an infrared vacuum label as $p_{\rm IR}\to-p_{\rm IR}+N-1$.
- **Chern–Simons term** (Euclidean, $d=3$): the weight carries the phase $\exp\big(\frac{ik}{4\pi}\int a\wedge da\big)$, the same orientation rule as the θ-term above.
- **K-matrix Chern–Simons theory (Sem II Week 10).** Weight $\exp\big(\frac{i}{4\pi}K_{IJ}\int a^I\wedge da^J\big)$ with the orientation of the Chern–Simons bullet above; the theory is bosonic iff $K$ has an even diagonal. Bracket $[a^I_i(\mathbf x),a^J_j(\mathbf y)]=2\pi i(K^{-1})^{IJ}\epsilon_{ij}\delta^2(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)$; a quasiparticle $l\in\mathbb{Z}^n$ carries flux $f=-2\pi K^{-1}l$ and charge $Q_l=-t^TK^{-1}l$, and $\sigma_{xy}=-t^TK^{-1}t\,e^2/2\pi$ (a response term $\frac k{4\pi}\int A\,dA$ means $\sigma_{xy}=k\,e^2/2\pi$, as for the θ-wall). Braiding and spin: $B(l,l')=e^{-2\pi i\,l^TK^{-1}l'}$ and, for a counterclockwise exchange (Lk $=+1$), $\theta_l=e^{-i\pi\,l^TK^{-1}l}$; positive $K$ has a clockwise edge, with $[\phi^I(x),\phi^J(y)]=i\pi(K^{-1})^{IJ}{\rm sgn}(x-y)$. Much of the quantum-Hall literature writes $-\frac{K}{4\pi}$, the mirror image, in which $\theta_l=e^{+i\pi l^TK^{-1}l}$. Labels: the toric code is $K=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\2&0\end{pmatrix}$ with $e=(1,0)$, $m=(0,-1)$, so that $e^am^b$ has $\theta=\omega^{ab}$; the double semion is $K={\rm diag}(2,-2)$ with $s=(0,1)$, $\theta_s=+i$, as in Sem II Week 7. For a quantum double $D(G)$ the anyons are pairs (conjugacy class, irreducible representation of a centralizer), with $\theta=\chi_\alpha(g)/\dim\alpha$.
- **Lattice θ-term.** It is built from the Villain field strength with cup products. It is $2\pi$-periodic only in the monopole-free (modified-Villain) theory, $dn=0$; with monopoles present the lattice $Q$ is in general not an integer (on an odd torus, $Q_{\rm lat}\in\mathbb{Z}$ for every $a$ if and only if $dn=0$). With $F=da-2\pi n$, $\frac1{8\pi^2}\sum F\cup F=\frac12\sum n\cup n-\frac1{4\pi}\sum(a\cup dn-dn\cup a)$, and a static monopole contributes the lattice Witten phase $e^{i(\theta/2\pi)\oint a}$ (Week 12 §6). Sem II Week 12 §5 seals this: in the modified Villain theory the θ-term is an integer and θ exactly $2\pi$-periodic; with a $\mathbb{Z}_N$ background, $Z(\theta+2\pi N;B)=\exp\big(\frac{2\pi i}N\frac{\int\mathcal P(B)}2\big)Z(\theta;B)$, and θ → θ + 2π alone is not a symmetry.
