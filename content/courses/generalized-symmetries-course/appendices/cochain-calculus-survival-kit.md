# Cochain Calculus Survival Kit

The reference for the lattice toolkit introduced in Semester I Week 2 and used in every problem set thereafter. Kept operational: finite linear algebra on a lattice, no point-set topology. Conventions match [[courses/generalized-symmetries-course/conventions]]; when a sign depends on orientation choices we follow Gorantla–Lam–Seiberg–Shao and Chen–Tata.

Read this once alongside Week 2, then keep it open as a lookup sheet.

---

## 1. Cells, chains, cochains

A hypercubic lattice $\Lambda$ is a **cell complex**: 0-cells (sites), 1-cells (links), 2-cells (plaquettes), ..., $p$-cells. Fix an orientation on every cell (links along $+\hat\mu$; a $p$-cell oriented by the ordered tuple of its directions $\hat\mu_1 < \dots < \hat\mu_p$).

- **$p$-chain** $c \in C_p$: a formal $\mathbb{Z}$-combination of oriented $p$-cells. "Where an extended object lives."
- **$p$-cochain** $f \in C^p(\Lambda,G)$: a $G$-valued function on oriented $p$-cells ($G = \mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{R}, U(1), \mathbb{Z}_N$). "A $p$-form field."
- **Pairing** $\langle f, c\rangle = \sum_i n_i\, f(\sigma_i)$ for $c = \sum_i n_i \sigma_i$. This is "$\int_c f$."

Fields live on cochains: a compact scalar is a $U(1)$ 0-cochain, a gauge field a $U(1)$ (or $\mathbb{R}$) 1-cochain, a $\mathbb{Z}_N$ 2-form background a $\mathbb{Z}_N$ 2-cochain.

---

## 2. Boundary $\partial$ and coboundary $d$

- **Boundary** $\partial: C_p \to C_{p-1}$: the oriented boundary of a cell, signs from orientation. $\partial^2 = 0$.
- **Coboundary** $d: C^p \to C^{p+1}$ defined by the **discrete Stokes theorem, taken as a definition**:
$$\langle df, c\rangle = \langle f, \partial c\rangle .$$
Then $d^2 = 0$ follows from $\partial^2 = 0$.

Concretely: $(df)$ on a $(p{+}1)$-cell = signed sum of $f$ over its boundary $p$-cells.
- 0-cochain (site field $\phi$): $(d\phi)_\ell = \phi_{y} - \phi_{x}$ on link $\ell = (x\to y)$. (Lattice gradient.)
- 1-cochain (link field $a$): $(da)_p = \sum_{\ell\in\partial p}\pm a_\ell$ around plaquette $p$. (Lattice curl / field strength.)
- 2-cochain: $(db)$ = signed sum over the faces of a cube. (Lattice "$dB$".)

**Codifferential** $\delta = \pm \star d\star$ lowers degree by one; it is the adjoint of $d$ under the pairing and lives naturally on the dual lattice (§4). The lattice Laplacian is $\Delta = \delta d + d\delta$.

---

## 3. Homology and cohomology

- **Closed / exact.** $f$ is closed if $df = 0$; exact if $f = dg$. Since $d^2=0$, exact $\Rightarrow$ closed.
- **Cohomology** $H^p(\Lambda, G) = \ker d\,/\,\mathrm{im}\,d$ = closed mod exact. Measures "closed cochains that are not the coboundary of anything" = global (topological) field configurations.
- **Homology** $H_p(\Lambda, G) = \ker\partial\,/\,\mathrm{im}\,\partial$ = cycles mod boundaries.

**On the $d$-torus $T^d$** (the case the course needs):
$$H^p(T^d, G) \cong G^{\binom{d}{p}}, \qquad b_p = \binom{d}{p}\ (\text{Betti numbers}).$$

| | $H^0$ | $H^1$ | $H^2$ | $H^3$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $T^2$ | $G$ | $G^2$ | $G$ | — |
| $T^3$ | $G$ | $G^3$ | $G^3$ | $G$ |

With $G = \mathbb{Z}_N$: replace $G$ by $\mathbb{Z}_N$ everywhere. $H^1(T^d,\mathbb{Z}_N) = \mathbb{Z}_N^d$ is the counting behind ground-state degeneracy (Sem II Wk 2).

**Generators.** $H^1(T^d,\mathbb{Z})$ is generated by the cochains "counting crossings of the $\hat\mu$-cycle"; $H_1(T^d,\mathbb{Z})$ by the $d$ non-contractible loops. The pairing $H^1 \times H_1 \to \mathbb{Z}$ is the winding/linking number.

## 4. The dual lattice and Poincaré duality

The dual lattice $\Lambda^*$ places a site at the center of each $d$-cell of $\Lambda$, and in general:
$$p\text{-cell of } \Lambda \ \longleftrightarrow\ (d{-}p)\text{-cell of } \Lambda^*.$$
So sites ↔ $d$-cells, links ↔ $(d{-}1)$-cells, etc. Orientation conventions (and the resulting signs in $\delta = \pm\star d\star$) follow GLSS.

**Poincaré duality** $H^p(T^d, G) \cong H_{d-p}(T^d, G)$: a closed $p$-cochain on $\Lambda$ is the same data as a $(d{-}p)$-cycle on $\Lambda^*$. This is why a monopole ($m = dn$, a 3-cochain in 4d) is a worldline (1-cycle) on the dual lattice, and a vortex in 2d is a point.

**Intersection pairing.** On $T^d$, $H_p \times H_{d-p} \to \mathbb{Z}$ (or $\mathbb{Z}_N$) by counting intersections. On $T^2$: $H_1 \times H_1 \to \mathbb{Z}_N$, the two cycles intersect once — this single "1" is the $\omega = e^{2\pi i/N}$ in the clock–shift algebra.

## 5. Poisson resummation — the duality engine

Every duality in the course is this identity, applied to an integer cochain:
$$\boxed{\ \sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}} f(n) = \sum_{w\in\mathbb{Z}} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dx\, f(x)\, e^{2\pi i w x}\ }$$

**Periodic-Gaussian form** (the one used constantly):
$$\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}} e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(\phi - 2\pi n)^2} \ \propto\ \sum_{w\in\mathbb{Z}} e^{-\frac{1}{2\beta} w^2 + i w \phi}.$$
Left: a periodic scalar with stiffness $\beta$. Right: a sum over integer "momenta"/charges $w$ with dual stiffness $1/\beta$. The coupling inverts, $\beta \leftrightarrow 1/\beta$ — the duality.

**Recipe (works for XY Wk 3, compact QED Wks 9/11, modified Villain Wk 12):**
1. Write the theory in Villain form with an integer cochain $n$.
2. Poisson-resum $n \to$ dual integer cochain $w$ on $\Lambda^*$.
3. The original field now appears linearly; integrating it out imposes a **constraint** ($\delta w = 0$, current conservation).
4. Solve the constraint: $w = d\tilde\phi$ off defects; the leftover defects (where $dn\neq0$) source the dual field.

## 6. Cup product (needed Sem II Weeks 6–7)

The lattice analogue of the wedge product, $\cup: C^p \times C^q \to C^{p+q}$ (Chen–Tata conventions). Properties:
- **Leibniz:** $d(x\cup y) = dx\cup y + (-1)^p\, x\cup dy$.
- **Graded commutativity up to exact terms:** $x\cup y - (-1)^{pq}\, y\cup x = d(\dots)$ via the higher cup product $\cup_1$. The non-commutativity is physical (it carries the anomaly), not a bookkeeping nuisance.
- **Pontryagin square** $\mathcal{P}(B)$ for $B \in C^2(\cdot,\mathbb{Z}_N)$: a refinement of $B\cup B$, valued in $\mathbb{Z}_{2N}$ ($N$ even) / $\mathbb{Z}_N$ ($N$ odd); it is the lattice object in the θ = π anomaly (Sem II Wk 6).

## 7. The Villain dictionary (quick lookup)

| Object | Cochain | Field strength / defect |
|---|---|---|
| Compact scalar $\theta$ | $U(1)$ 0-cochain | vortex $v = dn$, a 2-cochain (points in 2d) |
| $U(1)$ gauge field $a$ | $\mathbb{R}$ 1-cochain + integer $n\in C^2(\mathbb{Z})$ | monopole $m = dn$, a 3-cochain (points in 3d, loops in 4d) |
| $\mathbb{Z}_N$ $p$-form gauge field | $\mathbb{Z}_N$ $p$-cochain | flatness $da = 0$ mod $N$ |
| $\mathbb{Z}_N$ background | $\mathbb{Z}_N$ $(p{+}1)$-cochain $B$ | probes a $p$-form symmetry |

- **Villain scalar action:** $S = \frac{\beta}{2}\sum_\ell (d\theta - 2\pi n)^2$, $n\in C^1(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})$.
- **Villain gauge action:** $S = \frac{\beta}{2}\sum_p (da - 2\pi n)^2$, $n\in C^2(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})$.
- **Modified Villain:** promote $n$ to a dynamical $\mathbb{Z}$ gauge field and impose $dn = 0$ with a dual-lattice Lagrange multiplier — removes defects, makes symmetries and dualities *exact* (Sem II Wk 12).

## 8. Worked micro-examples (do these by hand once)

1. **$H^p(T^2,\mathbb{Z})$ from one cell each.** Minimal complex: 1 site, 2 links ($x,y$), 1 plaquette. $\partial(\text{plaq}) = 0$ (opposite edges cancel on the torus) ⇒ $H^2 = \mathbb{Z}$; $\ker\partial_1 = \mathbb{Z}^2$, no boundaries ⇒ $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}^2$; dualize for $H^1 = \mathbb{Z}^2$. Betti $(1,2,1)$. ✓
2. **Gradient is closed.** $(d\phi)_\ell = \phi_y-\phi_x$; check $d(d\phi) = 0$ around any plaquette (telescoping sum). ✓
3. **Poisson the Gaussian.** Verify $\sum_n e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(\phi-2\pi n)^2} \propto \sum_w e^{-w^2/2\beta + iw\phi}$ by completing the square inside the integral. ✓
4. **Torus intersection = 1.** The two 1-cycles of $T^2$ cross once; this is the seed of $ZX = \omega XZ$. ✓

## 9. Common pitfalls

- **Orientation signs.** Fix them once (per [[courses/generalized-symmetries-course/conventions]]) and never re-derive mid-calculation; most "duality is off by a sign" bugs are orientation bookkeeping.
- **Direct vs dual placement.** A field and its dual live on different lattices; a monopole is a *dual*-lattice object. Draw both lattices.
- **Villain vs cosine.** Villain is exact for duality; the cosine is not. Do not mix the two inside one derivation.
- **Cup non-commutativity.** Do not "simplify" $x\cup y$ to $\pm y\cup x$ — the difference is the anomaly.
