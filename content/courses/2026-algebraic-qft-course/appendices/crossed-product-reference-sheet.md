# Crossed-Product Reference Sheet

Compact reference for the crossed-product construction of Week 13 and the dressed entropy of Week 14.

## D.1 Setup

Inputs:
- A von Neumann algebra $\mathcal{M} \subset \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$.
- A $\sigma$-weakly continuous one-parameter automorphism group $\alpha: \mathbb{R} \to \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})$, $t \mapsto \alpha_t$.

Output: a new vN algebra $\mathcal{M} \rtimes_\alpha \mathbb{R}$ acting on $\mathcal{H} \otimes L^2(\mathbb{R})$.

## D.2 Definition

The **crossed product** $\mathcal{M} \rtimes_\alpha \mathbb{R}$ is the vN algebra on $\mathcal{H} \otimes L^2(\mathbb{R})$ generated by:

**Fiberwise representation**: for $a \in \mathcal{M}$,
$$
(\pi_\alpha(a)\,\xi)(s) := \alpha_{-s}(a)\,\xi(s), \qquad \xi \in \mathcal{H} \otimes L^2(\mathbb{R}).
$$

**Translation/clock unitary**: for $t \in \mathbb{R}$,
$$
(\lambda(t)\,\xi)(s) := \xi(s - t).
$$
i.e., $\lambda(t) = 1 \otimes U_t$ where $U_t$ is translation on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$.

## D.3 Covariance Relation

The defining relation:
$$
\lambda(t)\,\pi_\alpha(a)\,\lambda(t)^* = \pi_\alpha(\alpha_t(a)).
$$

The translation $\lambda(t)$ **inner-ifies** the automorphism $\alpha_t$: an outer automorphism of $\mathcal{M}$ becomes inner on $\mathcal{M} \rtimes_\alpha \mathbb{R}$.

*Physical reading (Week 13 §2.1):* $\pi_\alpha(a)$ is the **relational observable** "$a$ when the clock reads $s$" — the operator is rotated back by exactly the flow the clock has advanced. The crossed product is the algebra of constraint-invariant observables of (system + clock) under the total generator (flow generator + clock momentum). In gravity: gauge-invariant, gravitationally dressed observables.

## D.4 The Original Algebra Inside

$\pi_\alpha(\mathcal{M})$ is a proper vN subalgebra of $\mathcal{M} \rtimes_\alpha \mathbb{R}$. The crossed product is **strictly larger** than $\pi_\alpha(\mathcal{M})$, with the difference being the clock degrees of freedom.

## D.5 Dual Action

There is a canonical dual action $\theta: \hat{\mathbb{R}} \to \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M} \rtimes_\alpha \mathbb{R})$ given by
$$
\theta_r(\pi_\alpha(a)) = \pi_\alpha(a), \qquad \theta_r(\lambda(t)) = e^{itr}\,\lambda(t).
$$

The fixed-point subalgebra of $\theta$ is exactly $\pi_\alpha(\mathcal{M})$:
$$
(\mathcal{M} \rtimes_\alpha \mathbb{R})^{\hat{\mathbb{R}}} = \pi_\alpha(\mathcal{M}).
$$

This is the **first form of Takesaki duality** — the dual-action fixed-point algebra recovers $\mathcal{M}$.

## D.6 The Modular Crossed Product

When $\alpha = \sigma^\omega$ is the **modular flow** of a faithful normal state $\omega$ (our convention $\sigma^\omega_t = \mathrm{Ad}(\Delta^{-it})$):
$$
\hat{\mathcal{M}} := \mathcal{M} \rtimes_{\sigma^\omega} \mathbb{R}.
$$
This is the canonical case used throughout the course.

## D.7 Connes–Takesaki: Type Promotion

**Theorem 4.1 of Week 13. [Stated only — refs: Connes & Takesaki 1977; Takesaki Vol. III §VIII.3.]** Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a type III$_1$ factor with faithful normal state $\omega$. Then $\hat{\mathcal{M}} = \mathcal{M} \rtimes_{\sigma^\omega}\mathbb{R}$ is a **type II$_\infty$** factor.

More generally:
- $\mathcal{M}$ type III$_\lambda$ ($\lambda \in [0, 1]$) → $\hat{\mathcal{M}}$ type II$_\infty$.
- $\mathcal{M}$ type II$_\infty$ → $\hat{\mathcal{M}}$ type II$_\infty \otimes L^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ (no type promotion; $\sigma$ is inner).
- $\mathcal{M}$ type II$_1$ → $\hat{\mathcal{M}} \cong \mathcal{M} \otimes \mathcal{B}(L^2(\mathbb{R}))$.
- $\mathcal{M}$ type I → $\hat{\mathcal{M}} \cong \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}) \otimes L^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ (no type promotion; modular flow is inner).

**The type promotion happens only when the modular flow is outer on $\mathcal{M}$ — i.e., for type III.**

## D.8 The Dressed Trace

There is a faithful normal **semifinite** trace $\hat\tau$ on $\hat{\mathcal{M}}$ (type II$_\infty$), unique up to rescaling.

**Dual-action scaling.**
$$
\hat\tau \circ \theta_r = e^{-r}\,\hat\tau.
$$
The dual action scales the trace by $e^{-r}$. This is the structural reason the trace is unique up to multiplicative constant.

## D.9 Trace Formula in the Type-I Case

For $\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$ with state $\omega(\cdot) = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\cdot)$: after a unitary transformation, $\hat{\mathcal{M}} \cong \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}) \otimes L^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ with
$$
\hat\tau(a \otimes f) = \mathrm{Tr}(a)\,\int_{-\infty}^\infty f(s)\,e^{-s}\,ds.
$$
Semifinite: finite on $a \otimes f$ with $f$ exponentially decaying at large $s$, divergent on $1 \otimes 1$.

## D.10 Trace Formula in the Free-Field Rindler Case

For $\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{A}(W_R)$, $\sigma^\omega = $ boost (Bisognano–Wichmann), and $\hat{\mathcal{M}}$ on $\mathcal{F} \otimes L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$:
$$
\hat\tau(a) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-2\pi s}\,\langle 0_M \otimes \delta_s\,|\,a\,|\,0_M \otimes \delta_s\rangle\,ds
$$
(modulo regularization; the $|0_M \otimes \delta_s\rangle$ "states" are distributional, but the weighted integral is well-defined on a dense subspace of operators). The $e^{-2\pi s}$ weight corresponds to the Bisognano–Wichmann factor $2\pi$ in $\Delta_{W_R} = e^{-2\pi K}$.

## D.11 Dressed Entropy

A normal state $\hat\rho$ on $\hat{\mathcal{M}}$ has a density $\hat\rho_d$ relative to $\hat\tau$ (Radon–Nikodym):
$$
\hat\rho(x) = \hat\tau(\hat\rho_d\,x), \qquad x \in \hat{\mathcal{M}}.
$$
The **dressed entropy** is
$$
S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho) := -\hat\tau(\hat\rho_d\,\log\hat\rho_d).
$$
Well-defined up to a state-independent additive constant (from the $\hat\tau \to c\hat\tau$ rescaling ambiguity).

## D.12 Dressed-Entropy Difference Formula (Week 14 Theorem 3.1)

For two dressed states $\hat\rho_\omega, \hat\rho_\phi$ obtained from states $\omega, \phi$ on $\mathcal{M}$:
$$
\boxed{S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho_\omega) - S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho_\phi) = -S(\omega \| \phi) + \mathcal{B}(\omega, \phi),}
$$
where $S(\omega\|\phi)$ is the Araki–Uhlmann relative entropy (Week 7) and the **modular boundary term**:
$$
\mathcal{B}(\omega, \phi) = \omega(K_\phi) - \phi(K_\phi) = \mathrm{Tr}((\rho_\omega - \rho_\phi)\,K_\phi).
$$
**The boundary term uses the modular Hamiltonian $K_\phi = -\log\rho_\phi$ of the reference state $\phi$**, not of $\omega$.

Vanishes when $\omega = \phi$.

## D.13 Takesaki Duality (Full Form)

The crossed product of $\hat{\mathcal{M}}$ by the dual action $\theta$ recovers $\mathcal{M}$:
$$
\hat{\mathcal{M}} \rtimes_\theta \mathbb{R} \;\cong\; \mathcal{M} \otimes \mathcal{B}(L^2(\mathbb{R})).
$$
Modulo the tensor factor (which represents the "doubled clock" of taking two crossed products), the original algebra is recovered. This is the structural duality between $\mathcal{M}$ and its modular continuous core $\hat{\mathcal{M}}$.

## D.14 Common Pitfalls

1. **"Crossed product = gravitational dressing"**: NO, only in holographic settings (Witten 2022 identification, with $\alpha = $ ADM time flow as an additional input). The crossed product is the algebraic construction; the gravitational identification is a separate theorem of holographic QFT.

2. **"Dressed entropy = generalized entropy"**: holds only in holographic settings; equates $S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho) = A/(4G_N) + S_{\mathrm{out}} + \mathrm{const}$. Outside holography, $S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho)$ is just the trace entropy on the type II$_\infty$ algebra.

3. **"$\mathcal{M}$ embeds in $\hat{\mathcal{M}}$ with a trace"**: NO, the trace on $\hat{\mathcal{M}}$ restricted to $\pi_\alpha(\mathcal{M})$ is divergent on any non-zero element. The trace becomes finite only on operators that depend non-trivially on the clock.

4. **"Tensor product structure"**: the crossed product is $\mathcal{M} \otimes L^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ only when $\alpha$ is inner (type I, type II$_1$ trivial flow). For outer $\alpha$ (type III) the structure is *not* a tensor product — the dual action mixes $\mathcal{M}$ and the clock non-trivially.
