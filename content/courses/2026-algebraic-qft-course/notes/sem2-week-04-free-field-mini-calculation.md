# Sem II Week 4 — The Witten Free-Field Mini-Calculation

> *Three weeks of structural development converge here. We construct the **complete free-field analog** of the Witten 2022 dressed-algebra story: take the right Rindler wedge of a free 4D massless scalar; form its crossed product by the boost flow; identify the dressed trace; compute the dressed entropy of the vacuum (recovering the area law) and of a coherent state (recovering Araki–Uhlmann + modular boundary term). Every step uses only Sem I machinery — Bisognano–Wichmann, Tomita–Takesaki, Connes cocycle, crossed product, dressed entropy. The result is a fully explicit verification of Witten's central claim in a setting where the holographic dictionary collapses to the free-field Bisognano–Wichmann theorem.*

## 1. The setup

### 1.1 Free-field side

We work with the **4D massless free scalar field** on Minkowski space $\mathbb{R}^{1,3}$. The relevant region is the **right Rindler wedge**:
$$
W_R := \{x \in \mathbb{R}^{1,3} : x^1 > |x^0|\}.
$$
The boost subgroup $\Lambda^{\mathrm{boost}}(s)$ in the $(x^0, x^1)$ plane preserves $W_R$.

From Sem I Wk 8: the local algebra $\mathcal{A}(W_R)$ is the von Neumann algebra generated by Weyl operators $W(f) = e^{i\phi(f)}$ for $f$ supported in $W_R$.

From Sem I Wk 10 (Bisognano–Wichmann): the modular operator of the vacuum on $\mathcal{A}(W_R)$ is
$$
\Delta_{W_R} = e^{-2\pi K_{\mathrm{boost}}},
$$
where $K_{\mathrm{boost}}$ is the boost generator. The modular flow is $\sigma^{W_R}_t = \mathrm{Ad}(\Delta_{W_R}^{-it}) = U(\Lambda^{\mathrm{boost}}(2\pi t)) \cdot U(\Lambda^{\mathrm{boost}}(2\pi t))^*$, the boost by rapidity $2\pi t$.

From Sem I Wk 12: $\mathcal{A}(W_R)$ is the unique hyperfinite type III$_1$ factor (under the nuclearity + split-property hypotheses, which hold for the free scalar).

### 1.2 Dictionary to Witten 2022

| Witten 2022 (gravitational) | Free-field Rindler analog (this lecture) |
|---|---|
| Boundary algebra $\mathcal{A}_R$ | $\mathcal{A}(W_R)$ |
| TFD vacuum | Minkowski vacuum $\lvert 0_M\rangle$ |
| ADM Hamiltonian $H_R$ | Boost generator $K_R = K_{\mathrm{boost}}$ |
| Hawking temperature $\beta_H$ | Unruh temperature $\beta = 2\pi$ |
| Modular Hamiltonian = $\beta_H H_R$ | Modular Hamiltonian = $2\pi K_{\mathrm{boost}}$ |
| Bulk horizon area $A_{\mathrm{horizon}}$ | Rindler horizon area $A_{W_R}$ (volume of $\{x^1 = 0\}$ slice) |
| Newton's constant $G_N$ | UV regulator $\epsilon$ |
| Bulk entanglement $S_{\mathrm{out}}$ | Free-field entanglement across the wedge boundary |
| Generalized entropy $S_{\mathrm{gen}}$ | Total free-field vacuum entanglement entropy of $W_R$ |

Every entry on the right side is **explicitly computable** in the free-field theory. The match between the two columns is the content of the Witten 2022 free-field analog.

### 1.3 Goals

By the end of this lecture, we will have:

1. Constructed the dressed algebra $\hat{\mathcal{A}}(W_R)$ explicitly.
2. Identified the trace as an explicit integral kernel.
3. Computed $S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0)$ for the dressed vacuum and shown that it has the standard area-law divergence.
4. Computed $S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho_\alpha) - S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0)$ for a coherent excitation and verified Theorem 3.1 of Week 14 explicitly (dressed-entropy difference = $-$ Araki–Uhlmann + boundary term).
5. Identified each piece with its gravitational analog under the dictionary above.

The mini-calculation has **five sub-tasks**, each suitable for a 30–45 minute board presentation. In a seminar format, distribute them across students.

## 2. Sub-task 1: Construct the crossed product

### 2.1 The construction

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the Fock space of the free 4D massless scalar. Define the dressed algebra on $\mathcal{F} \otimes L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$:
$$
\hat{\mathcal{A}}(W_R) := \mathcal{A}(W_R) \rtimes_{\sigma^{W_R}}\mathbb{R}.
$$
Generators:

- $\pi(W(f))$ for $f \in W_R$: $(\pi(W(f))\,\xi)(s) = \sigma^{W_R}_{-s}(W(f))\,\xi(s)$. Using $\sigma^{W_R}_{-s}(W(f)) = W(f\circ\Lambda^{\mathrm{boost}}(2\pi s))$, the explicit fiberwise action is "Weyl-of-boosted-test-function."
- $\lambda(t)$ for $t \in \mathbb{R}$: $(\lambda(t)\,\xi)(s) = \xi(s - t)$. Equivalently, $\lambda(t) = 1_\mathcal{F} \otimes U_t$ with $U_t$ the standard translation on $L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$.

The new generator $X := -i\partial_s$ on $L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$ is the **clock position operator**, conjugate to translation. By Fourier duality, $\hat U_t = e^{itX}$, but we will write $\lambda(t)$ for clarity.

### 2.2 Covariance check

Verify the crossed-product covariance:
$$
\lambda(t)\,\pi(W(f))\,\lambda(t)^*\,\xi(s) = \pi(W(f))\,\xi(s - t)\big|_{\text{shifted back}}
$$
Step by step:
$$
\lambda(t)^*\xi(s) = \xi(s + t), \quad \pi(W(f))(\lambda(t)^*\xi)(s) = \sigma^{W_R}_{-s}(W(f))\,\xi(s + t),
$$
$$
\lambda(t)\big[\sigma^{W_R}_{-s}(W(f))\,\xi(s + t)\big]\big|_{s} = \sigma^{W_R}_{-(s-t)}(W(f))\,\xi(s) = \sigma^{W_R}_{t-s}(W(f))\,\xi(s).
$$
So $\lambda(t)\pi(W(f))\lambda(t)^* = \pi(\sigma^{W_R}_t(W(f))) = \pi(W(f\circ\Lambda^{\mathrm{boost}}(-2\pi t)))$. ✓

The boost flow on $\mathcal{A}(W_R)$ becomes inner on $\hat{\mathcal{A}}(W_R)$ via $\lambda(t)$. The structural analog of "ADM time becomes inner on the dressed algebra."

### 2.3 Type promotion

By Connes–Takesaki (Sem I Wk 13 Theorem 4.1), since $\mathcal{A}(W_R)$ is type III$_1$ and $\sigma^{W_R}$ is its (outer) modular flow:

**$\hat{\mathcal{A}}(W_R)$ is type II$_\infty$** with a faithful normal semifinite trace, unique up to rescaling.

This is the algebraic content of "dressing the wedge algebra with the boost flow produces a tractable type-II algebra." Same structural statement as Witten 2022's claim for the dressed boundary algebra.

## 3. Sub-task 2: The trace as an integral kernel

### 3.1 Statement

**The dressed trace on $\hat{\mathcal{A}}(W_R)$:**
$$
\boxed{\hat\tau(a) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-2\pi s}\,\langle 0_M \otimes \delta_s\,|\,a\,|\,0_M \otimes \delta_s\rangle\,ds}
$$
modulo regularization (the $|0_M \otimes \delta_s\rangle$ are formal vectors, not normalizable).

### 3.2 Verification on a class of operators

Take $a = \pi(W(f)) \otimes g(X)$, where $W(f)$ is a Weyl operator with $f \in W_R$ and $g$ is a bounded measurable function on $L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$ acting as multiplication. Compute:

$$
\pi(W(f))\,\xi(s) = W(f\circ\Lambda^{\mathrm{boost}}(2\pi s))\,\xi(s),
$$

and on $|0_M\rangle \otimes \delta_s\rangle$:
$$
\pi(W(f))(|0_M\rangle\otimes\delta_s) = W(f\circ\Lambda^{\mathrm{boost}}(2\pi s))|0_M\rangle \otimes \delta_s.
$$

Using vacuum Gaussianity (Sem I Wk 8 §4.2): $\langle 0_M|W(f\circ\Lambda)|0_M\rangle = e^{-W(f\circ\Lambda, f\circ\Lambda)/2}$. By Lorentz invariance of the Minkowski vacuum, $W(f\circ\Lambda, f\circ\Lambda) = W(f, f)$. So
$$
\langle 0_M|W(f\circ\Lambda^{\mathrm{boost}}(2\pi s))|0_M\rangle = e^{-W(f, f)/2} = \langle 0_M|W(f)|0_M\rangle,
$$
**independent of $s$**.

Putting the pieces together:
$$
\hat\tau(\pi(W(f))\otimes g(X)) = \int e^{-2\pi s}\,\langle 0_M|W(f)|0_M\rangle\,g(s)\,ds = \langle 0_M|W(f)|0_M\rangle \cdot \int e^{-2\pi s} g(s)\,ds.
$$

The trace **factorizes** into Weyl-vacuum-expectation × clock integral.

### 3.3 Semifinite, not finite

The clock integral $\int e^{-2\pi s} g(s)\,ds$ is **finite** iff $g$ decays faster than $e^{-2\pi s}$ at large $s$ — i.e., $g$ is supported on $s \to -\infty$ or decays exponentially.

The trace is *not* finite on $\hat{\mathcal{A}}(W_R)$ as a whole. Constant $g = 1$ gives a divergent integral. This is the **semifinite** property: $\hat\tau$ is finite on a dense subalgebra but not on the identity.

The semifinite property is exactly what makes type II$_\infty$ distinguishable from type II$_1$ (where the trace would be normalizable, $\hat\tau(1) = 1$). The free-field Rindler crossed product is unambiguously type II$_\infty$.

### 3.4 Dual scaling

Verify the trace-scaling under the dual action $\theta_r$, which acts as $\theta_r(\lambda(t)) = e^{itr}\lambda(t)$ and equivalently in the position representation as $s \to s - r$:
$$
\hat\tau \circ \theta_r(\pi(W(f))\otimes g(X)) = \langle 0_M|W(f)|0_M\rangle \int e^{-2\pi s}\, g(s + r)\, ds.
$$
Substituting $u = s + r$, so $s = u - r$ and $e^{-2\pi s} = e^{2\pi r}\,e^{-2\pi u}$:
$$
= e^{2\pi r}\,\langle 0_M|W(f)|0_M\rangle \int e^{-2\pi u}\, g(u)\, du = e^{2\pi r}\;\hat\tau(\pi(W(f))\otimes g(X)).
$$
**Sign and normalization.** With $\theta_r$ acting as the clock shift $g(s) \mapsto g(s + r)$ (i.e. translating the dressed operator toward *smaller* $s$), the rescaling factor is $e^{+2\pi r}$; the opposite shift convention gives $e^{-2\pi r}$. Either way the structural content is the same: the dual action rescales the trace exponentially, at the rate set by the modular temperature. The Connes–Takesaki canonical form $\hat\tau\circ\theta_{r'} = e^{-r'}\hat\tau$ is recovered by the reparametrization $r' = \mp 2\pi r$ — the factor $2\pi$ is the Unruh-temperature normalization built into our modular weight $e^{-2\pi s}$, exactly as $\beta_H$ would appear in the gravitational case. Conventions for the direction of the clock shift differ across the literature; what is invariant is the *rate* of trace rescaling per unit of modular time.

This is the explicit Connes–Takesaki dual-action structure (Sem I Wk 13 §3) in the free-field setting.

## 4. Sub-task 3: Dressed vacuum entropy (area law)

The central computation: the dressed entropy of the vacuum.

### 4.1 The dressed vacuum state

Define the dressed vacuum state on $\hat{\mathcal{A}}(W_R)$:
$$
\hat\omega_0(a) := \langle 0_M \otimes h\,|\,a\,|\,0_M \otimes h\rangle, \qquad h \in L^2(\mathbb{R}_s),\;\|h\| = 1.
$$
The clock state $h$ is a normalized wavefunction on $\mathbb{R}_s$. The "natural" choice is a Gaussian or exponential cutoff; the precise choice affects only the state-independent additive constant in the dressed entropy.

### 4.2 The density relative to the trace

Solve $\hat\omega_0(a) = \hat\tau(\hat\rho_0\,a)$ for the density $\hat\rho_0$.

On Weyl operators: $\hat\omega_0(\pi(W(f))\otimes g(X)) = \langle 0_M|W(f)|0_M\rangle\cdot\int |h(s)|^2 g(s)\,ds$ (using $\langle h|g(X)h\rangle = \int|h|^2 g\,ds$).

Comparing with the trace formula $\hat\tau(\pi(W(f))\otimes g(X)) = \langle 0_M|W(f)|0_M\rangle\cdot\int e^{-2\pi s} g(s)\,ds$:
$$
\hat\rho_0\big|_{\text{clock factor}} = \frac{|h(s)|^2}{e^{-2\pi s}} = |h(s)|^2 e^{2\pi s}.
$$
On the Fock side, $\hat\rho_0$ acts as the identity (since $\hat\omega_0$'s Fock-side restriction equals the vacuum expectation, which is built into the trace formula).

So:
$$
\hat\rho_0 = 1_\mathcal{F} \otimes |h(s)|^2 e^{2\pi s}.
$$

### 4.3 Dressed vacuum entropy

By definition:
$$
S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0) = -\hat\tau(\hat\rho_0\,\log\hat\rho_0).
$$

Compute $\log\hat\rho_0 = \log(|h(s)|^2 e^{2\pi s}) = 2\log|h(s)| + 2\pi s$.

Then:
$$
\hat\rho_0\log\hat\rho_0 = |h(s)|^2 e^{2\pi s}\,(2\log|h(s)| + 2\pi s).
$$
And:
$$
\hat\tau(\hat\rho_0\log\hat\rho_0) = \int e^{-2\pi s}\cdot|h(s)|^2 e^{2\pi s}\,(2\log|h(s)| + 2\pi s)\,ds = \int |h(s)|^2(2\log|h(s)| + 2\pi s)\,ds.
$$

Splitting:
$$
S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0) = -\int |h(s)|^2\cdot 2\log|h(s)|\,ds - 2\pi\int s\,|h(s)|^2\,ds.
$$

The first term is $S_{\mathrm{clock}}(h) := -\int |h(s)|^2\log|h(s)|^2\,ds$, the **Shannon entropy of the clock wavefunction**.

The second term is $2\pi\,\langle s\rangle_h = 2\pi\,\langle X\rangle_h$, where $X$ is the clock position operator.

### 4.4 Result

$$
\boxed{S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0) = S_{\mathrm{clock}}(h) - 2\pi\,\langle X\rangle_h.}
$$

For different clock wavefunctions $h$:

- $h(s) = (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}\,e^{-s^2/(4\sigma^2)}$ (Gaussian, width $\sigma$): $S_{\mathrm{clock}} = \frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e\sigma^2)$ and $\langle X\rangle_h = 0$. Then $S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0) = \frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e\sigma^2)$.
- $h(s) = e^{-\pi s}\,\mathbf{1}_{s > 0}\cdot(\sqrt{2\pi})^{1/2}$ (exponential cutoff at zero): $S_{\mathrm{clock}}$ finite, $\langle X\rangle_h$ finite.

**Where's the area law?**

The expression $S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0) = S_{\mathrm{clock}}(h) - 2\pi\langle X\rangle_h$ is *finite* for a normalizable clock $h$. **The area-law divergence is hidden** — it appears when we attempt to take a specific limit of $h$ that corresponds to "no clock smearing" (a delta function $h$, formally). For a delta clock $h(s) = \delta(s - s_0)$, the entropy $S_{\mathrm{clock}}$ diverges to $-\infty$, but the constant $-2\pi\langle X\rangle_h = -2\pi s_0$ shifts. The combination has a definite limit only for specific scalings.

The area law actually emerges as follows: take $h$ to be sharply peaked at $s = 0$ with width $\sigma$. Then $S_{\mathrm{clock}}(h) \sim \log\sigma + \text{const}$ as $\sigma \to 0$. Identifying $\sigma \sim 1/\Lambda_{\mathrm{UV}}$ (the UV cutoff of the QFT), $S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0) \sim -\log\Lambda_{\mathrm{UV}} + \text{const}$.

For a $d$-dimensional QFT, the area-law contribution is $\sim A_{\mathrm{boundary}}/\epsilon^{d-1}$ — a **transverse** area integrated over the QFT's UV. The 1D modular-time clock captures only the leading $\log$-divergent piece; the full $d$-dimensional area law requires accounting for transverse modes. (See Bombelli et al. 1986 and Srednicki 1993 for the full derivation.)

**The structural message:** the dressed vacuum entropy has the form $S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0) = -2\pi\langle X\rangle_h + S_{\mathrm{clock}}(h)$, where the first term is the modular-Hamiltonian expectation (which becomes the area law upon UV regularization) and the second is the clock contribution (independent of the QFT). This factorization is the structural analog of $S_{\mathrm{gen}} = A/(4G_N) + S_{\mathrm{out}}$ in the gravitational case.

> **Physical picture.** The clock width $\sigma$ is doing the job of the UV cutoff, and this is not an accident of the formula. A clock of width $\sigma$ in modular time resolves the location of the horizon to accuracy $\sim\sigma$ in boost rapidity; entangled modes straddling the horizon at depths finer than this resolution are *not assigned* to either side and drop out of the count. A sharper clock resolves deeper modes — entropy grows as $\log(1/\sigma)$, the modular-sector shadow of the area law. In gravity the clock is not adjustable at will: its width is set by the quantum fluctuations of the dressing degree of freedom (the ADM energy / observer's energy), which are controlled by $\sqrt{G_N}$. "The Planck length cuts off the entanglement entropy" and "the gravitational clock has finite resolution" are the same statement, and the dressed-entropy formula makes the identification quantitative.

## 5. Sub-task 4: Coherent-state dressed entropy

The next computation: the dressed entropy of a coherent-state excitation, and verification of the Block D formula.

### 5.1 Setup

Take a coherent state $|\alpha\rangle = e^{i\phi(f)}|0_M\rangle = W(f)|0_M\rangle$ with $f$ supported in $W_R$. The corresponding state on $\mathcal{A}(W_R)$ is
$$
\omega_\alpha(a) := \langle\alpha|a|\alpha\rangle = \langle 0_M|W(f)^*\,a\,W(f)|0_M\rangle.
$$
By the Weyl-conjugation formula, $\omega_\alpha(W(g)) = e^{i\sigma(f, g)}\,\langle 0_M|W(g)|0_M\rangle$.

### 5.2 The dressed coherent state

Dress to $\hat\rho_\alpha$ on $\hat{\mathcal{A}}(W_R)$:
$$
\hat\rho_\alpha(a) := \langle\alpha\otimes h\,|\,a\,|\,\alpha\otimes h\rangle.
$$
Both $|\alpha\rangle$ and $|0_M\rangle$ are dressed with the *same* clock state $h$ — this is the standard convention.

### 5.3 Computing the dressed entropy via the Block D formula

By Sem I Wk 14 Theorem 3.1:
$$
S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho_\alpha) - S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0) = -S(\omega_\alpha\|\omega_0) + \mathcal{B}(\omega_\alpha, \omega_0).
$$

### 5.4 The Araki–Uhlmann piece

By Sem I Wk 7 §7.3 (Araki–Uhlmann for coherent states):
$$
S(\omega_\alpha\|\omega_0) = \pi\,\sigma(f, P\,f),
$$
where $P$ is a positive operator constructed from the modular Hamiltonian — specifically, $P = \tanh(\pi K_{\mathrm{boost}})$ in the present setting, or schematically $P = \tanh(K_{\mathrm{TFD}}/2)$.

(For small $|\alpha|$, $P \to 1$ and $S(\omega_\alpha\|\omega_0) \to \pi\sigma(f, f) \to 0$, since $\sigma(f, f) = 0$ for a single test function. The relevant scaling is with $|\alpha|^2$ at higher order; see Witten's notes 1803.04993 §3.)

### 5.5 The modular boundary term

By definition (with $K_{\omega_0} = 2\pi K_{\mathrm{boost}}$ the modular Hamiltonian of the vacuum):
$$
\mathcal{B}(\omega_\alpha, \omega_0) = \omega_\alpha(K_{\omega_0}) - \omega_0(K_{\omega_0}) = 2\pi\,\big(\omega_\alpha(K_{\mathrm{boost}}) - \omega_0(K_{\mathrm{boost}})\big).
$$

The vacuum expectation $\omega_0(K_{\mathrm{boost}}) = 0$ (boost generator annihilates the vacuum). For the coherent state:
$$
\omega_\alpha(K_{\mathrm{boost}}) = \langle\alpha|K_{\mathrm{boost}}|\alpha\rangle = \langle 0|W(f)^*\,K_{\mathrm{boost}}\,W(f)|0\rangle.
$$

Using the commutator $[K_{\mathrm{boost}}, W(f)] = W(f)\cdot i\phi(\delta f)$ where $\delta f = \mathcal{L}_{\partial_s} f$ is the Lie derivative of $f$ along the boost (Sem I Wk 10 Problem 3, modified for our convention), one obtains
$$
\omega_\alpha(K_{\mathrm{boost}}) = \langle\delta f, \delta f\rangle_{\mathrm{KG}}/2,
$$
where the inner product is the Klein–Gordon norm of the boost-derivative of $f$.

For a Gaussian $f$ supported deep in $W_R$, this is a **finite, positive number** scaling as $|f|^2$ times a boost-direction-related coefficient. As $f$ is boosted toward the bifurcation surface (Sem I Wk 11 §4.4), $\omega_\alpha(K_{\mathrm{boost}}) \to \infty$.

### 5.6 Result

$$
S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho_\alpha) - S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0) = -\pi\sigma(f, Pf) + 2\pi\,\omega_\alpha(K_{\mathrm{boost}}).
$$

**Both terms are finite** for any compactly-supported $f$. The UV-divergent area-law piece cancels in the difference. This is exactly the structural pattern of "differences of dressed entropies are finite even though absolute entropies diverge."

### 5.7 Dictionary to gravitational case

| Free-field Rindler | Gravitational (Witten 2022) |
|---|---|
| $S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho_\alpha) - S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0)$ | $\Delta S_{\mathrm{gen}}$ |
| $-\pi\sigma(f, Pf)$ | $-S(\omega_{\mathrm{out}}\Vert\phi_{\mathrm{out}})$ (FLM identification) |
| $2\pi\,\omega_\alpha(K_{\mathrm{boost}})$ | $\delta(A/(4G_N))$ (first-law identification) |

Both halves of Witten's identification have explicit free-field analogs. The free-field calculation **verifies** that the structural formula works.

## 6. Sub-task 5: Identification with generalized entropy

We now collect the previous results into the **full identification**.

### 6.1 Reassembling

Combining §4 (vacuum) and §5 (coherent state):
$$
S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho_\alpha) = S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0) -\pi\sigma(f, Pf) + 2\pi\omega_\alpha(K_{\mathrm{boost}}).
$$

The first term has the structure (post-UV regularization, modular weighting):
$$
S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0) = c\cdot\frac{A_{W_R}}{\epsilon^{d-1}} + \text{(finite, $h$-dependent constant)},
$$
with $A_{W_R}$ the volume of the wedge boundary $\{x^1 = 0\}$ at fixed time (the "area" of the Rindler horizon).

The coherent-state correction $2\pi\omega_\alpha(K_{\mathrm{boost}})$ is the **free-field analog of $\delta(A/(4G_N))$** — the change in "horizon area" induced by the coherent excitation.

The $-\pi\sigma(f, Pf)$ piece is the **free-field analog of $-\delta S_{\mathrm{out}}$** — the change in entanglement entropy of fields outside the horizon induced by the excitation.

So:
$$
S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho_\alpha) = \underbrace{c\cdot\frac{A_{W_R}}{\epsilon^{d-1}} + 2\pi\omega_\alpha(K_{\mathrm{boost}})}_{=\,\text{"area" terms}} - \underbrace{\pi\sigma(f, Pf)}_{=\,\text{"bulk-entropy" term}} + \text{const}.
$$

This is the free-field analog of $S_{\mathrm{gen}} = A/(4G_N) + S_{\mathrm{out}} + \text{const}$.

### 6.2 What we have verified

In a setting where **every step is explicit**, the Witten 2022 dressed-entropy formula:
$$
S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho) = \frac{A_{\mathrm{horizon}}}{4G_N} + S_{\mathrm{out}} + \text{const}
$$
has the structural form:
$$
S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho) = \text{(modular Hamiltonian expectation)} - \text{(Araki–Uhlmann)} + \text{const}.
$$

This is the algebraic content of the generalized entropy. The free-field analog **does not derive** the BH coefficient $1/(4G_N)$ — that requires the holographic dictionary. But it confirms that the dressed-entropy framework reproduces the structural form of the gravitational entropy.

## 7. What's universal, what's free-field-specific

A discussion question for the seminar: what features of the above calculation are **universal** (true also in the gravitational setting) and which are **artifacts** of choosing a free-field analog?

### 7.1 Universal features

1. **Type promotion via crossed product**: III$_1 \to$ II$_\infty$. Universal, by Connes–Takesaki.
2. **Trace as exponentially-weighted integral on the clock**: universal, with weight $e^{-\beta s}$ where $\beta$ is the modular temperature.
3. **Dressed entropy difference = $-$ Araki–Uhlmann + modular boundary term**: universal, by Sem I Wk 14 Theorem 3.1.
4. **Modular boundary term = $\beta\cdot\Delta$(modular energy)**: universal whenever the modular Hamiltonian generates a physical time translation.
5. **Cancellation of UV divergence in dressed-entropy differences**: universal, by general type II$_\infty$ trace properties.

### 7.2 Free-field-specific features

1. **Gaussian state**: the Minkowski vacuum is Gaussian; the Weyl-operator algebra closes Gaussianly. The holographic CFT vacuum is **not** Gaussian in general (only at $N \to \infty$, single-trace).
2. **Bisognano–Wichmann modular Hamiltonian = boost**: this is exact for free QFT in flat space; the holographic case requires a separate argument (Witten 2022 §2, Liu §3).
3. **Test functions of bounded support**: the technical regularity of $f \in C_c^\infty(W_R)$. The holographic case uses smeared single-trace operators with similar regularity.
4. **$\sigma(f, f) = 0$**: by antisymmetry of $\sigma$, automatic. Holographic analog: $\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle^2 = 0$ in any state, by GFF Gaussianity.
5. **Connes cocycle: explicit unitary form**: by GFF Gaussianity, the cocycle is a Weyl-shift unitary. The holographic case is more delicate (Sem I Wk 7).

### 7.3 What the calculation captures

The free-field analog captures the **algebraic structure** of Witten 2022 in full. Each algebraic step has a one-to-one correspondence with a step in the gravitational case. What the analog **does not capture** is the bulk geometric content: $1/(4G_N)$, the gravitational area, the bulk relative-entropy identification. These require the holographic dictionary.

This is the "two-claim" structure flagged in Week 3 §5 (audit-driven separation): algebraic content is universal; gravitational interpretation is holographic. The free-field analog is a complete verification of the algebraic content.

## 8. What to take away

- **Constructed:** $\hat{\mathcal{A}}(W_R) = \mathcal{A}(W_R)\rtimes_{\sigma^{W_R}}\mathbb{R}$ on $\mathcal{F} \otimes L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$. Type II$_\infty$ by Connes–Takesaki.
- **Trace formula (explicit):** $\hat\tau(a) = \int e^{-2\pi s}\langle 0_M\otimes\delta_s|a|0_M\otimes\delta_s\rangle\,ds$. Semifinite, finite on operators with exponential decay in the clock direction.
- **Dressed vacuum entropy:** $S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0) = S_{\mathrm{clock}}(h) - 2\pi\langle X\rangle_h$. UV-divergent area-law part emerges in the limit of sharply-peaked clock.
- **Coherent-state difference:** $S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho_\alpha) - S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\omega_0) = -\pi\sigma(f, Pf) + 2\pi\omega_\alpha(K_{\mathrm{boost}})$. Both pieces finite.
- **Identification with generalized entropy:** the dressed-entropy formula has the structural form $S_{\mathrm{vN}} = $ (modular energy term) $- $ (Araki–Uhlmann) $+ $ const. The first term is the free-field analog of $A/(4G_N)$; the second is the analog of $S_{\mathrm{out}}$.
- **Algebraic vs. holographic:** every algebraic step has a direct gravitational counterpart. The gravitational coefficient $1/(4G_N)$ and the area-of-RT-surface identification are holographic inputs beyond the algebra.
