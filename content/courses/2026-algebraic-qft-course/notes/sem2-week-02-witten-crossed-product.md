# Sem II Week 2 — Witten's Construction: Gauging the Modular Flow

> *Week 1 set up the large-$N$ side: boundary single-trace algebra $\mathcal{A}_R$ is type III$_1$; its modular flow equals the ADM time translation $\alpha^{H_R}_t$. Today we form the crossed product $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R = \mathcal{A}_R \rtimes_{\mathrm{ADM}}\mathbb{R}$, verify the type promotion to II$_\infty$ via Connes–Takesaki, and identify the dressed trace as an explicit integral kernel. The construction is structurally identical to the Block D modular crossed product (Sem I Wk 13); the new physics input is the **identification with gauging the ADM constraint** — i.e., the algebraic procedure of including the gravitational constraint that the ADM Hamiltonian generate a gauge orbit, not a physical observable.*

## 1. The semiclassical issue

Why does the boundary CFT, considered as a quantum theory dual to bulk gravity, **not** include the ADM Hamiltonian as an inner element of its algebra?

### 1.1 Diffeomorphism invariance and the constraint

In any gravitational theory, **time translations are gauge.** General covariance treats the ADM Hamiltonian $H_{\mathrm{ADM}}$ as a *constraint*: physical states $|\Psi\rangle$ must satisfy $H_{\mathrm{ADM}}|\Psi\rangle = 0$ (Wheeler–DeWitt), or equivalently the only physical observables are those that commute with $H_{\mathrm{ADM}}$.

In AdS/CFT, this manifests on the boundary as follows. At finite $N$, the boundary CFT has a finite-dimensional energy operator $H_R$ and the algebra $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_R)$ is type I; $H_R \in \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_R)$ is an *inner* element. At infinite $N$ (classical gravity), $H_R$ has an unbounded spectrum on the GFF Hilbert space, and the natural large-$N$ boundary algebra $\mathcal{A}_R$ is type III$_1$.

**Crucially: $H_R$ is *not* an element of $\mathcal{A}_R$ at $N = \infty$.** It generates an *outer* automorphism $\alpha^{H_R}_t$. This is the algebraic content of "the bulk Hamiltonian is gauge."

### 1.2 The structural problem

In a type III$_1$ algebra, there is no trace, no density matrix, no canonical entropy. The boundary algebra $\mathcal{A}_R$ at large $N$ has these defects (Sem I Wk 12). But the bulk gravity it is dual to has a perfectly well-defined notion of **generalized entropy**:
$$
S_{\mathrm{gen}} = \frac{A_{\mathrm{horizon}}}{4 G_N} + S_{\mathrm{out}}.
$$
The boundary algebra is *missing* the algebraic structure to match this bulk formula. Witten's question:

> What additional structure does the boundary algebra need so that its **algebraic entropy** matches the bulk **generalized entropy**?

The answer is the crossed product.

### 1.3 The fix: gauge $H_R$ by crossing

The idea: $H_R$ is outer on $\mathcal{A}_R$ (a structural feature of the gauge constraint). Make it inner by **crossing**: include the unitary $\lambda(t) := e^{itH_R}$ as a new generator of an enlarged algebra. The crossed product is
$$
\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R \;:=\; \mathcal{A}_R \rtimes_{\alpha^{H_R}} \mathbb{R},
$$
the algebra generated by $\mathcal{A}_R$ and the clock unitaries $\lambda(t)$.

On $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R$, the time translation $\alpha^{H_R}_t$ is now an *inner* automorphism (implemented by $\lambda(t)$). By Connes–Takesaki, $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R$ is **type II$_\infty$** with a faithful normal semifinite trace.

This is the central construction of Witten 2022. The procedure is algebraic; its physical interpretation as "gauging the ADM constraint" is the Witten-specific input.

> **Physical picture: why gauging *adds* rather than removes.** "Gauging a symmetry" usually suggests *shrinking* the observable algebra (keep only invariants), yet the crossed product looks like an *enlargement* (adjoin $\lambda(t)$ and a clock). The two views are the same operation seen before and after solving the constraint. Start from the kinematical system (boundary QFT) $\otimes$ (clock with momentum conjugate to time): the constraint is $\hat C = H_R + p_{\rm clock}$, and the gauge-invariant subalgebra of the *enlarged* kinematical algebra — everything commuting with $\hat C$ — is precisely $\mathcal{A}_R \rtimes \mathbb{R}$ (Sem I Wk 13, relational-observable picture). Without the clock, the invariant subalgebra would be only the time-independent observables — far too small to describe anything. The clock is what allows gauge-invariant *relational* statements ("the field when the clock reads $s$") to exist. So the dressed algebra is simultaneously smaller than the kinematical algebra (constraint imposed) and richer than the naive boundary algebra (clock adjoined) — and it is the second comparison that produces the trace.

## 2. The algebraic construction

### 2.1 Setup

Let $\mathcal{A}_R$ be the type III$_1$ boundary single-trace algebra at large $N$, with TFD vacuum $|\mathrm{TFD}\rangle$ and modular flow $\sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}_t = \alpha^{H_R}_{\beta_H t}$ (Week 1 Theorem 5.1).

We construct the crossed product on the doubled Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_R \otimes L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$, where $s$ is the new "clock" variable.

### 2.2 The crossed-product algebra

**Definition 2.1.** The **gravitationally dressed boundary algebra** is
$$
\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R \;:=\; \mathcal{A}_R \rtimes_{\sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}}\mathbb{R}
$$
acting on $\mathcal{H}_R \otimes L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$, generated by:

- Fiberwise representation: $\pi(a)\xi(s) = \sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}_{-s}(a)\,\xi(s)$ for $a \in \mathcal{A}_R$.
- Translation: $\lambda(t)\,\xi(s) = \xi(s - t)$.

The covariance relation (Sem I Wk 13 §2.3):
$$
\lambda(t)\,\pi(a)\,\lambda(t)^* = \pi(\sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}_t(a)) = \pi(\alpha^{H_R}_{\beta_H t}(a)).
$$

Equivalently, the unitary $\lambda(t)$ implements the modular flow inside $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R$. Identifying $\beta_H t = u$, the boundary ADM time translation $\alpha^{H_R}_u$ becomes inner on $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R$:
$$
\alpha^{H_R}_u(a) = \lambda(u/\beta_H)\,a\,\lambda(u/\beta_H)^*.
$$
So $\lambda(u/\beta_H) = e^{i u H_R / \beta_H}$ acts as the inner implementer of ADM time translation. **This is the algebraic content of gauging $H_R$.**

### 2.3 Witten's physical interpretation

Witten 2022 interprets the construction as follows. At finite $N$:
- $H_R \in \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_R)$ is an inner Hamiltonian.
- $e^{itH_R}$ is in the algebra; time evolution is inner.

At $N = \infty$:
- $\mathcal{A}_R$ is type III$_1$.
- $H_R$ is outer; cannot be reconstructed from $\mathcal{A}_R$ alone.
- The crossed product **explicitly adjoins a representative** of $e^{itH_R}$ (namely $\lambda(t)$) to the algebra.

The new algebra $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R$ is morally "the boundary CFT with the ADM Hamiltonian put back in by hand." From the bulk side, this corresponds to **including the ADM constraint** — i.e., the requirement that physical states transform appropriately under bulk time translation.

### 2.4 The dual action and Takesaki duality

The dual action $\theta_r: \hat{\mathbb{R}} \to \mathrm{Aut}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R)$:
$$
\theta_r(\pi(a)) = \pi(a), \qquad \theta_r(\lambda(t)) = e^{itr}\,\lambda(t).
$$

The fixed-point algebra under $\theta$ is the original boundary algebra:
$$
(\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R)^{\hat{\mathbb{R}}} = \pi(\mathcal{A}_R).
$$

**Takesaki duality** (Sem I Wk 13 §3): one can recover $\mathcal{A}_R$ from $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R$ via the dual action. The dressed algebra contains the original algebra as a "constrained subalgebra" (fixed points of the dual flow).

## 3. Type promotion via Connes–Takesaki

### 3.1 The theorem

**Theorem 3.1 (Connes–Takesaki applied to $\mathcal{A}_R$). [Stated only — refs: Sem I Wk 13 Thm 4.1; Connes & Takesaki 1977.]** *If $\mathcal{A}_R$ is a type III$_1$ factor and $\sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}$ is its modular flow on the TFD state, then*
$$
\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R \;=\; \mathcal{A}_R \rtimes_{\sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}}\mathbb{R}
$$
*is a type II$_\infty$ factor with a faithful normal semifinite trace $\hat\tau$, unique up to rescaling.*

This is the structural input. At the algebraic level, it is exactly the Sem I Wk 13 theorem. The new physics is that "$\mathcal{A}_R$" here is the large-$N$ boundary single-trace algebra, and "$\sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}$" is the ADM time translation.

### 3.2 Trace scaling

The trace transforms under the dual action by
$$
\hat\tau \circ \theta_r = e^{-r}\,\hat\tau.
$$
The factor $e^{-r}$ is the universal Connes–Takesaki feature: the dual action *rescales* the trace by an exponential factor. This is what makes the trace unique up to a scalar.

In our convention (matching Witten's via $t \to t$ rescaling), the dressed entropy is well-defined up to an additive constant from the trace rescaling ambiguity (Sem I Wk 14 §2.3). The constant cancels in **differences** of entropies.

### 3.3 Why this isn't trivial

A common student question: "Doesn't the crossed product just add a $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ factor? Why does it change the type?"

The answer is that the modular flow is **outer**. If we crossed $\mathcal{A}_R$ by an *inner* automorphism (one implemented by a unitary already in $\mathcal{A}_R$), the result would be a tensor product $\mathcal{A}_R \otimes L^\infty(\mathbb{R})$, no type change. But the modular flow on a type III$_1$ algebra is genuinely outer — that's a defining feature of type III (Sem I Wk 6 §6, Wk 12 §2). So crossing by it creates a genuinely new algebra with a different type.

**The structural reason** the type promotes: type III$_1$ algebras have no trace because all projections are infinite (Sem I Wk 12). The crossed product breaks this by adjoining $\lambda(t)$ such that the new algebra has *finite* projections (mainly in the $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ direction). The Connes–Takesaki theorem says the result is exactly type II$_\infty$.

## 4. Identifying the trace

The trace $\hat\tau$ on $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R$ has an explicit characterization in the holographic setting. We give two formulations.

### 4.1 As an integral kernel

In the free-field Rindler analog (Sem I Wk 14 §5), the trace is:
$$
\hat\tau(a) = \int e^{-2\pi s}\,\langle 0_M \otimes \delta_s\,|\,a\,|\,0_M \otimes \delta_s\rangle\,ds.
$$
The factor $e^{-2\pi s}$ comes from $\Delta_{W_R} = e^{-2\pi K}$ (Bisognano–Wichmann); $\beta_H$ in the gravitational case plays the role of $2\pi$.

In the gravitational case, the trace formula reads:
$$
\hat\tau(a) = \int e^{-\beta_H s}\,\langle \mathrm{TFD} \otimes \delta_s\,|\,a\,|\,\mathrm{TFD} \otimes \delta_s\rangle\,ds.
$$

(Modulo the same regularization caveats as Sem I Wk 14: the $|\mathrm{TFD}\otimes\delta_s\rangle$ are not normalizable states; the integral with the $e^{-\beta_H s}$ weight is well-defined on a dense subspace of operators.)

The interpretation: the trace is the **thermal expectation** in the TFD vacuum, weighted by the modular factor $e^{-\beta_H s}$ over the clock dimension $s$.

### 4.2 As a Plancherel weight

An equivalent formulation: identify the GNS Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_R \otimes L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$ via Fourier transform on the second factor. After Fourier:
$$
\hat\tau(a) = \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_R}(\rho_{\mathrm{TFD}}^{-1/2}\,a_{\mathrm{0-mode}}\,\rho_{\mathrm{TFD}}^{1/2}),
$$
where $a_{\mathrm{0-mode}}$ is the zero-mode of $a$ under the dual action and $\rho_{\mathrm{TFD}} = e^{-\beta_H H_R}/Z$ is the formal Gibbs density. This formula is **formal** in type III$_1$ ($\rho_{\mathrm{TFD}}$ doesn't exist as a density matrix), but it captures the structural meaning of the trace.

### 4.3 What the trace measures

For an operator $a = \pi(b)\,\lambda(t)$ with $b \in \mathcal{A}_R$ and $\lambda(t)$ a clock unitary, the trace evaluates as
$$
\hat\tau(\pi(b)\lambda(t)) = e^{-i\beta_H t \cdot 0} \cdot \delta(t)\cdot \omega_{\mathrm{TFD}}(b)
$$
schematically. The trace is **non-zero only on operators with trivial clock dependence**, and on those it reduces to the TFD expectation value.

This is the explicit content of "the dressed algebra has a trace that the original algebra doesn't" — the trace lives on the clock-extended sector, not on the original $\mathcal{A}_R$.

## 5. Worked example: free-field Rindler

The full structural picture in the free-field analog. This is the running example for Block 1 Weeks 2–4.

### 5.1 Setup

Replace the holographic CFT$_d$ with a free 4D massless scalar field. The boundary subregion is replaced by the right Rindler wedge $W_R$. The boundary algebra $\mathcal{A}_R$ becomes $\mathcal{A}(W_R)$ (Sem I Wk 8).

The TFD vacuum is replaced by the Minkowski vacuum, which is the TFD of the boost Hamiltonian at Unruh temperature $\beta = 2\pi$ (Sem I Wk 15 §6).

The modular flow $\sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}$ is replaced by the boost flow $\sigma^{W_R}_t$ with rapidity $2\pi t$ (Bisognano–Wichmann, Sem I Wk 10).

### 5.2 The crossed product

$$
\hat{\mathcal{A}}(W_R) := \mathcal{A}(W_R) \rtimes_{\sigma^{W_R}}\mathbb{R}
$$
on $\mathcal{F} \otimes L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$, where $\mathcal{F}$ is the Fock space.

**Type promotion (Theorem 3.1 applied):** $\hat{\mathcal{A}}(W_R)$ is type II$_\infty$ since $\mathcal{A}(W_R)$ is type III$_1$ and the boost is its outer modular flow.

### 5.3 The trace

The trace formula (Sem I Wk 14 §5.2):
$$
\hat\tau(a) = \int e^{-2\pi s}\,\langle 0_M \otimes \delta_s\,|\,a\,|\,0_M \otimes \delta_s\rangle\,ds.
$$
The $e^{-2\pi s}$ factor is the Bisognano–Wichmann modular weight, corresponding to Unruh temperature $\beta = 2\pi$.

### 5.4 Verifying the dual scaling

A subtlety is worth spelling out, because a naive check appears to *contradict* the scaling law. For a monomial $a = \pi(b)\lambda(t)$ with $b \in \mathcal{A}(W_R)$, the dual action gives only a phase:
$$
\theta_r(a) = \pi(b)\,e^{itr}\lambda(t) = e^{itr}\,a,
$$
so $\hat\tau(\theta_r(a)) = e^{itr}\hat\tau(a)$ — where is the $e^{-r}$?

The resolution: $\hat\tau$ vanishes on every monomial with $t \neq 0$ (§4.3), so the phase is invisible on the support of the trace, and monomials with $t = 0$ are $\theta$-invariant — the naive check is vacuous. The scaling law is a statement about *trace-class* elements, which are not monomials but operators of the form $x^* x$ with $x$ built from clock-smeared combinations $\int dt\,g(t)\,\pi(b_t)\lambda(t)$. On such elements the dual action shifts the clock variable, $\theta_r$ acting as $s \mapsto s - r$ in the position representation, and the weight $e^{-2\pi s}\,ds$ in the trace kernel picks up exactly the factor $e^{-2\pi r}$ — which is $e^{-r}$ in the convention where the dual variable is rescaled by $2\pi$ (the Plancherel normalization; Takesaki Vol. III §VIII.3 for the careful version). Problem 4 asks for this computation in detail. The lesson: trace identities on crossed products must always be tested on the trace-class ideal, never on the generating monomials.

### 5.5 The dressed-algebra structure

For an operator $b \in \mathcal{A}(W_R)$ tensored with a clock-localized function $g \in L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$:
$$
(\pi(b) \otimes g(X))\,\xi(s) = b\cdot g(s)\cdot\xi(s) \quad (\text{schematically; with } \sigma_{-s} \text{ rotation hidden inside}).
$$

The trace evaluates as
$$
\hat\tau(\pi(b) \otimes g(X)) = \omega_0(b)\cdot \int e^{-2\pi s}\,g(s)\,ds.
$$
This is finite iff $g$ decays exponentially at large $s$ — the semifinite property.

## 6. The bulk picture (preview)

A brief preview of what the gravitational dressing means in the bulk, to be elaborated in Week 3:

### 6.1 What is the auxiliary $L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$?

The auxiliary $L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$ has a bulk interpretation as **the conjugate variable to the ADM Hamiltonian** — i.e., an "observer's clock" or a "time-of-flight" coordinate in the bulk.

In the free-field Rindler model, $s$ is the **Rindler time** of a uniformly accelerated observer. In the gravitational case, $s$ is the **boundary time** of an observer at the AdS boundary.

The crossed product introduces this clock as an additional degree of freedom. Physically, it represents an external observer whose clock reading we condition on. The dressed observables are "observables seen by an observer reading clock value $s$."

### 6.2 ADM-time gauge

The dressing corresponds to picking a **gauge** for the bulk time-translation diffeomorphism: dressing by $\alpha^{H_R}_t$ means "observe relative to an external clock that runs at the ADM rate." Different choices of clock (different bulk time gauges) give different but equivalent dressings.

This is the algebraic content of "gravitational dressing" — the crossed product encodes the gauge-fixing of $H_{\mathrm{ADM}}$. Witten 2022 makes this connection precise; we will not develop the full bulk argument.

### 6.3 What we get

The dressed algebra $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R$ is:
- Type II$_\infty$ (Theorem 3.1).
- Has a faithful normal semifinite trace $\hat\tau$.
- Contains the original boundary algebra $\mathcal{A}_R$ as the fixed-point subalgebra of the dual action.
- Has a well-defined entropy notion (Week 3).

The Witten 2022 claim — and the punchline of the entire Block 1 — is that the dressed entropy of $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R$ equals the bulk generalized entropy. Week 3 derives this.

## 7. Comparison with the abstract crossed product (Sem I Wk 13)

We summarize the parallels:

| | Sem I Wk 13 (abstract) | Sem II Wk 2 (Witten) |
|---|---|---|
| Algebra | $\mathcal{M}$ (vN algebra) | $\mathcal{A}_R$ (boundary single-trace at large $N$) |
| State | faithful normal $\omega$ | TFD vacuum |
| Flow | modular $\sigma^\omega$ | modular = ADM time $\alpha^{H_R}_{\beta_H t}$ |
| Crossed product | $\mathcal{M} \rtimes_{\sigma^\omega}\mathbb{R}$ | $\mathcal{A}_R \rtimes_{\mathrm{ADM}}\mathbb{R}$ |
| Type promotion | III$_1 \to$ II$_\infty$ | III$_1 \to$ II$_\infty$ |
| Trace | $\hat\tau$ unique up to scaling | same |
| Dual scaling | $\hat\tau \circ \theta_r = e^{-r}\hat\tau$ | same |
| Original algebra inside | $\pi(\mathcal{M}) = (\hat{\mathcal{M}})^{\hat{\mathbb{R}}}$ | $\pi(\mathcal{A}_R) = (\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R)^{\hat{\mathbb{R}}}$ |

**The Sem II construction is literally a special case of Sem I's,** with the abstract modular flow specialized to the ADM time flow at large $N$. **The new physics input** is the identification "modular = ADM" — i.e., that the boundary CFT's modular structure encodes the bulk gravitational time-translation. Once that identification is in hand, the algebraic part is identical to Sem I Wk 13.

## 8. What to take away

- **Witten's construction (Witten 2022 §3):** form the crossed product $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R = \mathcal{A}_R \rtimes_{\mathrm{ADM}}\mathbb{R}$ on $\mathcal{H}_R \otimes L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$. The ADM Hamiltonian becomes inner on the dressed algebra via the clock unitary $\lambda(t) = e^{itH_R/\beta_H \cdot \beta_H} = e^{itH_R}$.
- **Stated only:** $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_R$ is type II$_\infty$ with a faithful normal semifinite trace $\hat\tau$, unique up to rescaling (Connes–Takesaki, Sem I Wk 13).
- **Trace formula** (free-field analog, derived explicitly): $\hat\tau(a) = \int e^{-2\pi s}\,\langle 0_M \otimes \delta_s\,|\,a\,|\,0_M\otimes\delta_s\rangle\,ds$. In the gravitational case, $\beta_H$ replaces $2\pi$.
- **Algebraic identity vs. holographic identification:** the algebraic crossed product is structural (Block D). The physical identification with gauging the ADM constraint is Witten's input. The two parts are distinct logical layers.
- **The auxiliary $L^2(\mathbb{R}_s)$** is the conjugate variable to the ADM Hamiltonian — an "observer's clock" or "boundary time" coordinate.
- **Dual action $\theta_r$** scales the trace by $e^{-r}$; fixed-point algebra is $\pi(\mathcal{A}_R)$. Takesaki duality says the construction is invertible.
- **Free-field Rindler model** runs in parallel throughout: $\mathcal{A}(W_R) \rtimes_{\mathrm{boost}}\mathbb{R}$ has all the same features with explicit computations.

## 9. Looking ahead

Week 3 turns the dressed algebra into the **dressed entropy**: $S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\hat\rho) = -\hat\tau(\hat\rho_d \log\hat\rho_d)$. Witten 2022's central claim is that this equals the generalized entropy $A/(4G_N) + S_{\mathrm{out}}$ modulo a state-independent additive constant. The derivation uses the Block D dressed-entropy formula (Sem I Wk 14 Theorem 3.1) plus the holographic identification of the modular boundary term with the area term. Week 4 verifies every step in the free-field Rindler analog.
