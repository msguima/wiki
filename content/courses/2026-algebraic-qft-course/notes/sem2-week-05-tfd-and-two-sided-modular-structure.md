# Sem II Week 5 — The TFD as a Type III$_1$ KMS State

> *Block 2 turns to the standard representation appropriate to a two-sided eternal black hole. The TFD vector is cyclic and separating for each **one-sided** algebra. Its modular operator is generated by the two-sided Liouvillean $\beta_H(H_R-H_L)$ in the finite-dimensional regulator, while the induced automorphism on the right algebra is right-boundary time translation. At large $N$ the one-sided factors act on one TFD GNS Hilbert space, which need not factorize as $\mathcal H_R\otimes\mathcal H_L$. This distinction is essential: the TFD is not cyclic and separating for the algebra generated by both mutually commuting factors when that joint algebra is $\mathcal B(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{TFD}})$.*

## 1. The two-sided eternal black hole

### 1.1 The geometry

The **maximally extended AdS-Schwarzschild black hole** in $(d+1)$ dimensions has the metric
$$
ds^2 = -f(r)\,dt^2 + \frac{dr^2}{f(r)} + r^2\,d\Omega_{d-1}^2,
\qquad
f(r)=1-\frac{\mu}{r^{d-2}}+\frac{r^2}{\ell^2},
$$
where $\mu=r_h^{d-2}(1+r_h^2/\ell^2)$ so that $f(r_h)=0$. Extending through $r=r_h$ gives the full Penrose diagram, with four regions:

- **Right exterior** (region I): an asymptotically AdS region with a CFT$_d$ on its boundary at $r \to \infty$.
- **Left exterior** (region III): a mirror copy of the right exterior, with a second CFT$_d$ on its boundary.
- **Future interior** (region II): the part of the geometry behind the horizon, inside the future light cone of the bifurcation surface.
- **Past interior** (region IV): the mirror of region II.

The two exteriors are **causally disconnected** — no timelike curve connects them — but they are **bulk-geometrically connected** through the Einstein–Rosen bridge.

### 1.2 The bifurcation surface

At the center of the Penrose diagram, the Killing vector $\partial_t$ vanishes. This codimension-2 locus is the **bifurcation surface** $\Sigma$, topologically $S^{d-1}$ (the angular sphere at $r = r_h, t = 0$).

The bifurcation surface plays two roles:

- It is the intersection of the future and past horizons; the null horizons themselves emanate from it.
- It is the **edge of the entanglement wedge** of either boundary: the surface anchoring the Ryu–Takayanagi formula.

### 1.3 The Hawking temperature

The surface gravity at the horizon gives the **Hawking temperature**:
$$
T_H = \frac{f'(r_h)}{4\pi}
=\frac{(d-2)+d r_h^2/\ell^2}{4\pi r_h}.
$$
For a large black hole, $r_h\gg\ell$, this becomes $T_H\sim d\,r_h/(4\pi\ell^2)$.

The **inverse Hawking temperature** $\beta_H = 1/T_H$ enters as the periodicity of imaginary time in the Euclidean continuation of the geometry. It is the universal scale in the two-sided story.

### 1.4 Maldacena's duality

**Holographic identification (Maldacena 2001). [Stated only.]** *The two-sided eternal AdS-Schwarzschild black hole is dual, in the semiclassical regime, to two copies of the boundary CFT prepared in the thermofield-double state*
$$
|\mathrm{TFD}_{\beta_H}\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{Z(\beta_H)}}\sum_n e^{-\beta_H E_n/2}\,|n\rangle_R \otimes |n\rangle_L,
$$
*where $|n\rangle_R, |n\rangle_L$ are energy eigenstates of the right and left CFTs (with the same Hamiltonian spectrum).*

This is the **foundational identification** of the entire Sem II program. The two-sided geometry has a single state-level dual: the boundary CFTs in the TFD.

## 2. The two boundary algebras

### 2.1 Setup

CPW do not begin with an algebra of operators “at one fixed time.” They take the large-$N$ thermal correlation functions of subtracted single-trace operators, build the corresponding GNS Hilbert space, and then take the von Neumann closure of bounded functions of finite products. Equivalently, one may use an appropriate boundary time band/timelike tube whose causal completion captures the exterior algebra. In this week we suppress CPW's subscript $0$ until the Hamiltonian/clock is adjoined in Week 6, and denote the two GNS algebras by:

- $\mathcal{A}_R$: the right thermal GNS von Neumann algebra generated by the large-$N$ single-trace data;
- $\mathcal{A}_L$: its left counterpart/commutant in the two-sided representation, once the CPW duality identification is imposed.

These are the **two-sided boundary single-trace algebras**. In the strict microcanonical large-$N$ GNS regime analyzed by CPW, before the Hamiltonian/clock extension is formed, each is type III$_1$ on the appropriate Hilbert space (Week 1 §4.2). This is CPW's limiting construction, not Witten's separate perturbative $1/N$ algebra.

### 2.2 Causal complementarity

The right and left boundaries are causally disconnected (§1.1). At the operator-algebra level, this means:
$$
[\mathcal{A}_R, \mathcal{A}_L] = 0.
$$
Any right-boundary operator commutes with any left-boundary operator. This is the algebraic content of "the two CFTs are non-interacting."

In holographic terms: causal complementarity on the boundary corresponds to **bulk causal separation** between the two asymptotic regions through the wormhole.

### 2.3 The joint algebra is not the modular pair

The algebra generated by both sides is
$$
\mathcal{A}_R \vee \mathcal{A}_L := (\mathcal{A}_R \cup \mathcal{A}_L)'',
$$
acting on $\mathcal H_{\mathrm{TFD}}$. In the standard factorial situation, wedge duality identifies $\mathcal A_L=\mathcal A_R'$, and then $\mathcal A_R\vee\mathcal A_L=\mathcal B(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{TFD}})$. The TFD vector is not separating for this joint algebra. Tomita–Takesaki theory is applied to the pair $(\mathcal A_R,\Omega_{\mathrm{TFD}})$, or symmetrically to $(\mathcal A_L,\Omega_{\mathrm{TFD}})$, not to the joint algebra.

At finite $N$, before taking the GNS/large-$N$ limit, one may use the regulator $\mathcal H_R\otimes\mathcal H_L$ and $\mathcal B(\mathcal H_R)\otimes1$. The tensor-product notation below belongs to this regulator.

## 3. The TFD as cyclic-separating

### 3.1 Statement

**TFD standardness. [Finite-dimensional proof; large-$N$ statement imported from CPW.]** *In the finite-dimensional regulator, a full-Schmidt-rank TFD vector is cyclic and separating for $\mathcal B(\mathcal H_R)\otimes1$. In the large-$N$ GNS representation, the TFD vector is cyclic and separating for the one-sided factor $\mathcal A_R$.*

By symmetry, the same is true for the left one-sided algebra. It is not true for the joint algebra $\mathcal A_R\vee\mathcal A_L$ when that algebra equals $\mathcal B(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{TFD}})$.

### 3.2 The Tomita–Takesaki data

By Sem I Wk 5 (Tomita–Takesaki theorem) and cyclic-separating from §3.1, we obtain:

- A **modular operator** $\Delta_{\mathrm{TFD}}$ on $\mathcal{H}_R \otimes \mathcal{H}_L$, positive self-adjoint.
- A **modular conjugation** $J_{\mathrm{TFD}}$, antiunitary.
- A **modular flow** $\sigma_t^{\mathrm{TFD}}(a) := \Delta_{\mathrm{TFD}}^{-it}\,a\,\Delta_{\mathrm{TFD}}^{it}$ on $\mathcal{A}_R$.

The TFD vector is **KMS at $\beta = +1$** for $\sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}$, in our upper-strip convention (Sem I Wk 6).

### 3.3 Modular Hamiltonian = $\beta_H(H_R - H_L)$

In finite dimensions (Sem I Wk 15 §1.2), the modular operator of the TFD is
$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{TFD}} = \rho_R \otimes \rho_L^{-1} = e^{-\beta_H(H_R - H_L)},
$$
and the modular Hamiltonian is
$$
K_{\mathrm{TFD}} = -\log\Delta_{\mathrm{TFD}} = \beta_H(H_R - H_L).
$$

The partition functions cancel between $\rho_R$ and $\rho_L^{-1}$. Thus there is no $Z^{-2}$ factor in $\Delta$ and no $2\log Z$ term in the standard Liouvillean.

In the large-$N$ holographic setting, the same identification holds (CPW §2, hypothesis-explicit):
$$
\boxed{K_{\mathrm{TFD}} = \beta_H\,(H_R - H_L).}
$$

**The crucial structural feature.** The operator implementing the modular automorphism on the standard Hilbert space is the two-sided Liouvillean $\beta_H(H_R-H_L)$. The formal one-sided expression $-\log\rho_R=\beta_HH_R+\log Z$ generates the same automorphism on right observables in the type-I regulator, but it is not the standard modular operator on the doubled space.

> **Physical picture: the standard generator versus its formal halves.** The modular Hamiltonian $K=-\log\Delta$ of a standard pair is a genuine self-adjoint operator on the GNS Hilbert space. What generally fails in the type-III setting is a reduced density matrix $\rho_R$ and a decomposition of $K$ into separately defined self-adjoint operators $\beta_HH_R$ and $-\beta_HH_L$ on that same Hilbert space. Stress-tensor expressions for such one-sided pieces may first exist only as quadratic forms and can have divergent fluctuations at the bifurcation surface. The two-sided Liouvillean $\beta_H(H_R-H_L)$ is the well-defined standard-space implementer; $H_R$ survives safely as shorthand for the **restricted right automorphism**, not necessarily as an element or separate operator of the limiting algebra. The finite-dimensional TFD formulas, where both halves exist, are a regulator that makes this distinction visible rather than erasing it.

### 3.4 The two-sided boost interpretation

In the bulk, the Killing vector $\partial_t$ that generates time translation on the right boundary also generates *backward* time translation on the left boundary (by the time-reversal symmetry of the maximally extended geometry). This is the **two-sided boost**: $H_R$ on the right minus $H_L$ on the left.

In the free-field Rindler analog (Sem I Wk 15 §2), the analog is the **Lorentz boost** in the $(x^0, x^1)$-plane: it generates positive time translation in $W_R$ and negative time translation in $W_L$, with the bifurcation surface $\{x^0 = x^1 = 0\}$ fixed.

This identification is a holographic analogue of Bisognano–Wichmann in the stationary eternal-black-hole setting. We use it as part of the holographic dictionary; it is not a general theorem for arbitrary states or boundary regions.

## 4. KMS at the Hawking temperature

### 4.1 The KMS condition

The modular flow $\sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}_t = \mathrm{Ad}(\Delta_{\mathrm{TFD}}^{-it}) = \mathrm{Ad}(e^{i\beta_H t(H_R - H_L)})$ acts on $a \in \mathcal{A}_R$ as **time evolution by $\beta_H t$ on the right**, with the left-side rotation acting trivially because $a \in \mathcal{A}_R$ commutes with $H_L$.

So for $a \in \mathcal{A}_R$:
$$
\sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}_t(a) = e^{i\beta_H t\,H_R}\,a\,e^{-i\beta_H t\,H_R} = \alpha^{H_R}_{\beta_H t}(a).
$$

Identifying $u = \beta_H t$, the modular flow $\sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}$ on $\mathcal{A}_R$ is the **ADM time translation** with rapidity $u$. The modular time $t$ is the dimensionless time, related to ADM time by $u = \beta_H t$.

### 4.2 KMS at $\beta_H$

The TFD is KMS at modular $\beta = 1$ for $\sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}$ (Sem I Wk 6 Theorem 2.1). Translating to ADM time $u = \beta_H t$, this is KMS at inverse temperature $\beta_H$ for the ADM time flow:
$$
\omega_{\mathrm{TFD}}(a\,\alpha^{H_R}_u(b)) \text{ extends to } 0 \le \mathrm{Im}\,u \le \beta_H, \text{ with } \omega_{\mathrm{TFD}}(a\,\alpha^{H_R}_{u+i\beta_H}(b)) = \omega_{\mathrm{TFD}}(\alpha^{H_R}_u(b)\,a).
$$

**The TFD vacuum is KMS at the Hawking temperature for ADM time translation on the right boundary.** This is the holographic Unruh effect.

### 4.3 One automorphism, two representations

The formulas $\beta_HH_R+\log Z$ and $\beta_H(H_R-H_L)$ should not be called two different modular Hamiltonians of the same Tomita pair:

- **Density-matrix language:** $-\log\rho_R=\beta_HH_R+\log Z$ is an operator on $\mathcal H_R$ that generates the right Gibbs automorphism.
- **Standard representation:** $-\log\Delta_{\mathrm{TFD}}=\beta_H(H_R-H_L)$ acts on $\mathcal H_R\otimes\mathcal H_L$ and annihilates the TFD vector.

They induce the same automorphism on $\mathcal A_R$ because the left term lies in the commutant. At large $N$, the automorphism survives even though neither one-sided density matrix nor a tensor-product factorization need exist.

In the finite-dimensional regulator, the standard modular Hamiltonian $\beta_H(H_R-H_L)$ acts on the doubled Hilbert space. In the large-$N$ algebraic limit, the robust statement is instead that the standard Liouvillean implements the modular automorphism on the GNS Hilbert space; a literal tensor product need not survive.

## 5. Modular conjugation: ER=EPR

### 5.1 The modular conjugation $J_{\mathrm{TFD}}$

By Sem I Wk 5, the Tomita–Takesaki theorem gives an antiunitary modular conjugation $J_{\mathrm{TFD}}$ on $\mathcal{H}_R \otimes \mathcal{H}_L$.

**Theorem 5.1 (Tomita commutant theorem). [Stated only — ref: Sem I Wk 5.]** *For the standard pair $(\mathcal A_R,\Omega_{\mathrm{TFD}})$,*
$$
J_{\mathrm{TFD}}\mathcal A_RJ_{\mathrm{TFD}}=\mathcal A_R'.
$$

This theorem identifies the right algebra with its commutant. The further identification $\mathcal A_R'=\mathcal A_L$ is a **duality assumption** about the two-sided representation—automatic in the finite-dimensional TFD model, and supplied by wedge duality in the Rindler example. It is not a consequence of cyclicity and separatingness alone. With that extra input, $J_{\mathrm{TFD}}$ exchanges the two represented sides.

### 5.2 Algebraic ER=EPR

The structural picture:

- $\mathcal{A}_R, \mathcal{A}_L$ are causally disconnected (cannot communicate).
- $|\mathrm{TFD}\rangle$ is cyclic-separating for both.
- $J_{\mathrm{TFD}}$ realizes a canonical isomorphism between them.

These facts are a useful **algebraic shadow** of the eternal-black-hole construction: they organize the two exteriors and their correlations without presupposing a tensor factorization. They do not give an if-and-only-if criterion for a bulk bridge. Standard pairs with the same commutant relation occur already for opposite Rindler wedges in ordinary QFT, where no dynamical Einstein–Rosen bridge is being inferred.

**Maldacena–Susskind's heuristic (ER=EPR):** the TFD/eternal-black-hole example motivates a relation between entanglement and bulk connectivity. Here $J\mathcal A_RJ=\mathcal A_L$ is a necessary structural consistency check in the chosen two-sided representation, not a sufficient reconstruction theorem for a wormhole.

### 5.3 What ER=EPR is not

This is **not** the claim that *any* entanglement generates a bulk wormhole geometry. In the eternal-black-hole setting, the modular data are compatible with the known geometric dual; recovering that geometry additionally requires the holographic dictionary, a suitable semiclassical state, and bulk reconstruction.

The Sem II Block 3 (Liu lectures) discusses the algebraic ER=EPR proposal in more detail.

## 6. Worked example: free-field Rindler-Rindler

The two-sided analog of the Sem I Wk 15 §2 free-field Rindler model. Same machinery, two sides.

### 6.1 Setup

Take the 4D massless free scalar field on Minkowski space. The commuting wedge algebras generate
$$
\mathcal{A}(W_R) \vee \mathcal{A}(W_L) \subset \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F})
$$
on the Fock space $\mathcal F$. Reeh–Schlieder makes $|0_M\rangle$ cyclic and separating for **each one-sided wedge algebra**. If wedge duality gives $\mathcal A(W_L)=\mathcal A(W_R)'$, their join is $\mathcal B(\mathcal F)$; then the vacuum cannot be separating for that joint algebra.

### 6.2 Minkowski vacuum is the TFD

**Theorem 6.1 (Bisognano–Wichmann; one-sided standard pair). [Stated only — ref: Sem I Wk 10.]** *For $(\mathcal A(W_R),|0_M\rangle)$, the modular group is the Lorentz-boost group with the conventional $2\pi$ normalization, and modular conjugation maps the right wedge to its commutant.*

In a Rindler-mode regulator one often writes the mnemonic
$$
|0_M\rangle = \prod_\omega \frac{1}{\sqrt{Z_\omega}}\sum_{n_\omega} e^{-\pi n_\omega \omega}\,|n_\omega\rangle_R \otimes |n_\omega\rangle_L.
$$
Mode by mode, this is a TFD at inverse boost temperature $2\pi$. In the continuum the displayed infinite product is formal: the Minkowski and Rindler Fock representations are not related by a literal Hilbert-space tensor factorization with a normalizable product vector. The intrinsic statement is the KMS/modular statement. In a standard representation, the corresponding modular Liouvillean is conventionally written
$$
K_{0_M} = 2\pi(K_R - K_L),
$$
where the notation records the opposite boost orientations on the two wedges.

### 6.3 KMS at $\beta = 2\pi$

The Minkowski vacuum is KMS at modular $\beta = 1$ for $\sigma^{0_M}_t = \mathrm{Ad}(e^{2\pi i t(K_R - K_L)})$, which after rescaling $s = 2\pi t$ is KMS at $\beta = 2\pi$ for the boost flow with rapidity $s$.

This is Bisognano–Wichmann plus the Unruh interpretation. Each one-sided restriction is KMS at inverse boost temperature $2\pi$; the formal TFD expansion is a useful mode-by-mode picture of those correlations.

### 6.4 Modular conjugation: PCT

By Bisognano–Wichmann (Sem I Wk 10 §6), the modular conjugation is **PCT**:
$$
J_{0_M} = \Theta \cdot R_\perp(\pi),
$$
where $\Theta$ is the antiunitary CPT operator and $R_\perp(\pi)$ is a $\pi$-rotation in the perpendicular directions $(x^2,x^3)$ in $3+1$ spacetime dimensions—that is, $d=3$ spatial dimensions in the course convention. This swaps $W_R$ with $W_L$; identifying the image algebra with the full commutant also uses wedge duality.

In 2D ($d = 1$), $J_{0_M} = \Theta$ (no perpendicular rotation needed), and $\Theta$ acts as $(x^0, x^1) \to (-x^0, -x^1)$, swapping $W_R \leftrightarrow W_L$.

### 6.5 The free-field analog mirror

The dictionary between the gravitational and free-field analog settings:

| CPW (gravitational) | Free-field Rindler-Rindler |
|---|---|
| Right CFT boundary | Right Rindler wedge $W_R$ |
| Left CFT boundary | Left Rindler wedge $W_L$ |
| Bifurcation surface (BH horizon) | Bifurcation surface $\{x^0 = x^1 = 0\}$ |
| TFD at $\beta_H$ | Minkowski vacuum: KMS at boost inverse temperature $2\pi$; formal mode TFD |
| $H_R - H_L$ (two-sided ADM) | $K_R - K_L$ (two-sided boost) |
| Modular Hamiltonian $\beta_H(H_R - H_L)$ | Modular Hamiltonian $2\pi(K_R - K_L)$ |
| Modular flow = ADM time difference | Modular flow = Lorentz boost |
| Modular data used in the ER=EPR discussion | Lorentz-boost modular flow and wedge duality |

The right column supplies a controlled analog for the modular and KMS statements. It does **not** verify the gravitational interpretation: replacing the boost generator by an ADM charge is a holographic input, and the existence of Rindler modular data alone does not imply a wormhole.

## 7. Why $-H_L$? An invariance argument

A pedagogically helpful aside: why does the **difference** $H_R - H_L$ appear in the modular Hamiltonian, rather than just $H_R$ on the right?

### 7.1 TFD invariance

The TFD is invariant under the combined right-minus-left time translation:
$$
(H_R-H_L)|\mathrm{TFD}\rangle=0.
$$
**Proof.** $H_R$ acts as $\sum_n E_n |n\rangle_R\langle n|_R$, similarly $H_L$. On the TFD,
$$
H_R|\mathrm{TFD}\rangle = \sum_n \frac{E_n e^{-\beta_H E_n/2}}{\sqrt{Z}}\,|n\rangle_R\otimes|n\rangle_L = H_L|\mathrm{TFD}\rangle,
$$
since the sum is symmetric in the index $n$ and $H_L$ acts on the left with the same eigenvalue. Hence $(H_R - H_L)|\mathrm{TFD}\rangle = 0$. $\square$

So $H_R - H_L$ annihilates the TFD, while $H_R + H_L$ does not. The difference is the **boost-like** generator that fixes the TFD (and the bifurcation surface in the bulk picture).

### 7.2 Modular Hamiltonian fixes the modular state

By Tomita–Takesaki, $\Delta_\Omega\Omega=\Omega$, and hence $K_\Omega\Omega=-\log\Delta_\Omega\,\Omega=0$. So whatever implements the modular flow of the one-sided TFD standard pair must annihilate the TFD vector. The natural candidate is $\beta_H(H_R-H_L)$, consistent with the explicit finite-dimensional calculation in §3.3.

This is a *consistency check* on the identification $K_{\mathrm{TFD}} = \beta_H(H_R - H_L)$, not a derivation — but it's a useful sanity check.

### 7.3 Bulk interpretation

In the bulk, the Killing vector field $\xi = \partial_t$ that generates ADM time on the right boundary continues across the horizon (with sign flip) to generate ADM time *backwards* on the left boundary. This is the **boost Killing vector** of the maximally extended geometry, which fixes the bifurcation surface.

On the boundary CFT, this Killing vector projects to $H_R$ on the right and $-H_L$ on the left, i.e., the bulk Killing vector is dual to $H_R - H_L$. This is the **boost-symmetry** of the eternal BH.

So the standard-space modular Hamiltonian $\beta_H(H_R-H_L)$ has a clean bulk interpretation: it implements the boundary action of the bulk boost Killing field. Calling this a “holographic Bisognano–Wichmann” relation is useful shorthand, but it is an identification in the eternal-black-hole code subspace, not a general theorem for arbitrary holographic regions or states.

## 8. Discussion: two-sided modular structure

### 8.1 What the TFD knows

The TFD vector defines correlated restrictions to $\mathcal A_R$ and $\mathcal A_L$. The standard pair $(\mathcal A_R,\Omega_{\mathrm{TFD}})$ determines:

1. The **right CFT's reduced state**: a thermal Gibbs state at $\beta_H$ (the bulk Hawking effect from the perspective of an observer outside the BH).
2. The **modular Hamiltonian** $\beta_H(H_R - H_L)$, which equals the bulk Killing flow at the bifurcation surface.
3. The commutant $J\mathcal A_RJ=\mathcal A_R'$; identifying it with $\mathcal A_L$ requires the two-sided duality input.

In the eternal-black-hole example these data match Hawking thermality and boost symmetry. The bridge interpretation comes from the holographic state/geometry dictionary, not from Tomita–Takesaki theory by itself.

### 8.2 What's the same as Block 1

The Block 2 setup is the two-sided extension of Block 1:

| Block 1 (single-sided) | Block 2 (two-sided) |
|---|---|
| Algebra $\mathcal{A}_R$ alone | Two commuting represented sides $\mathcal A_R,\mathcal A_L$ |
| Cyclic-separating: one-sided thermal state | TFD vector standard for each one-sided algebra |
| Right modular automorphism: formally generated by $\beta_HH_R$ | Standard-space implementer: $\beta_H(H_R-H_L)$ |
| Modular flow on $\mathcal{A}_R$: ADM time on right | Same (when restricted to $\mathcal{A}_R$) |
| Type III$_1$: yes | Yes (each side) |
| KMS at $\beta_H$: yes | Yes (each side) |

For the **single-side restriction to $\mathcal{A}_R$ alone**, the two stories are *equivalent* — the modular flow on $\mathcal{A}_R$ generated by $\beta_H(H_R - H_L)$ equals the one generated by $\beta_H H_R$ (since $H_L$ commutes with $\mathcal{A}_R$).

The new structural content in Block 2 is the **two-sided representation**: how the one-sided algebra and its commutant are related, when the commutant can be identified with the other boundary algebra, and how dressing works in that setting.

### 8.3 What's new in Block 2

Beyond Block 1, Block 2 will address:

1. **The CPW dressing** (Week 6): both algebras dressed in a way that respects the two-sided structure.
2. **Entropy of the relevant right/dressed algebra** (Week 7): in CPW's construction this entropy, evaluated on the stated semiclassical states, matches the generalized entropy of the black-hole bifurcation surface up to the trace-normalization constant. It is not an entropy of the full joint algebra.
3. **Bell–CHSH between two sides** (Week 8): the algebraic content of "the TFD is maximally entangled across the bifurcation horizon."

These are the new things the two-sided structure unlocks.

## 9. What to take away

- **Two-sided eternal BH (Maldacena 2001):** dual to the boundary CFTs in the TFD state at the Hawking temperature.
- **TFD as cyclic-separating** (stated only, hypothesis-explicit): for each of $\mathcal A_R$ and $\mathcal A_L$ at large $N$. This licenses Tomita–Takesaki for either one-sided standard pair, not for their join.
- **Modular Hamiltonian = $\beta_H(H_R - H_L)$:** the two-sided modular Hamiltonian, projecting to $\beta_H H_R$ when restricted to $\mathcal{A}_R$ alone.
- **KMS at $\beta_H$** for ADM time translation on either side. Holographic Unruh effect.
- **Modular conjugation = PCT/boost-reflection** in the free-field analog; its identification with the other boundary algebra in gravity is conditional on the two-sided duality input.
- **Free-field Rindler analog:** an explicit check of modular flow, KMS thermality, and wedge duality—not a derivation of bulk connectivity.
- **Why $-H_L$:** the TFD is annihilated by $H_R - H_L$, not by $H_R$ alone. This is the algebraic content of "the TFD is boost-invariant."

## 11. Problem set

**Core problems.**

**1. TFD reduced state.** Verify directly that $\mathrm{Tr}_L(|\mathrm{TFD}_{\beta_H}\rangle\langle\mathrm{TFD}_{\beta_H}|) = e^{-\beta_H H_R}/Z(\beta_H)$ in finite dimensions. Conclude that the right CFT's reduced state is the Gibbs state at $\beta_H$.

**2. Modular operator of the TFD.** In finite dimensions, verify $\Delta_{\mathrm{TFD}}=\rho_R\otimes\rho_L^{-1}$ with $\rho_R=\rho_L=e^{-\beta_HH}/Z$. Compute $K_{\mathrm{TFD}}=-\log\Delta_{\mathrm{TFD}}$ and show that the two normalization constants cancel, leaving $\beta_H(H_R-H_L)$.

**3. The TFD is annihilated by $H_R - H_L$.** Verify $(H_R - H_L)|\mathrm{TFD}_{\beta_H}\rangle = 0$ from the explicit form. Why does this not hold for $H_R + H_L$?

**4. Two-sided modular flow.** Show that for $a \in \mathcal{A}_R \otimes 1$, the modular flow $\sigma^{\mathrm{TFD}}_t(a) = e^{i\beta_H t(H_R - H_L)}\,a\,e^{-i\beta_H t(H_R - H_L)}$ reduces to $e^{i\beta_H t H_R}\,a\,e^{-i\beta_H t H_R}$ (one-sided ADM time on the right). Where does $H_L$ go?

**5. Modular conjugation in a wedge.** Using the Bisognano–Wichmann statement supplied in the notes, show that in two dimensions the geometric action of $J$ sends $W_R$ to $W_L$. State clearly which additional assumption turns this geometric inclusion into the equality $\mathcal A(W_L)=\mathcal A(W_R)'$.

**Starred problems.**

**6\*. KMS at $\beta_H$.** Take a thermal two-point function in a 2D CFT on the nonrotating BTZ boundary and verify its KMS continuation with inverse temperature $\beta_H=2\pi\ell^2/r_h$. Distinguish KMS exchange of operator order from simple periodicity. (*Hint:* use the conformal map from the plane to the thermal cylinder.)

**7\*. Read CPW §2.** Identify the precise hypotheses CPW use for the two-sided large-$N$ structure. Compare with our statement in §3.1.

**8\*. Read Maldacena 2001 §§1–3.** Identify the precise argument for the eternal BH ↔ TFD duality. Where does the holographic dictionary enter, and where is it taken as input?

**9\*. Boundary Killing vector.** Sketch why the restriction of the standard modular flow to $\mathcal A_R$ is the right ADM-time automorphism with parameter $u=\beta_Ht$. Use $\beta_H(H_R-H_L)$ in the finite regulator, and explain why writing $\beta_HH_R$ for the restricted action does not assert that a separate $H_R$ exists in the limiting GNS algebra. (*Hint:* the boundary projection of $\partial_t$ generates ADM time on the right; $\beta_H$ comes from the surface gravity.)

**Project problems.**

**10. Connection to Liu §VIII.3.** Liu (arXiv:2510.07017) §VIII.3 discusses a conditional algebraic ER=EPR proposal. Write a five-page exposition comparing CPW's two-sided modular structure with Liu's distinction between classical and quantum-volatile bulk descriptions. Record the semiclassical and $\alpha'$ qualifications rather than replacing the proposal by the generic identity $J\mathcal AJ=\mathcal A'$.

**11. Higher-genus generalization.** Sketch what changes in the two-sided story if we replace the eternal AdS-Schwarzschild by an eternal AdS black hole at higher genus (e.g., a torus boundary). What's the analog of the TFD? How does the modular Hamiltonian change?
