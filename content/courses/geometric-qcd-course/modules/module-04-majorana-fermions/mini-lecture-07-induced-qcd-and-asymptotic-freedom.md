## The Question

In what sense do the slides claim to induce planar QCD?

## Notation

- $Z[1]$: the Elfin determinant on the trivial loop (the "partition function").
- $\lambda\propto Z[1]^2$: the induced 't Hooft coupling.
- $m\to\infty$: the large-mass limit; $Z[1]^2\to 0$; $\lambda\to 0$.
- Induced QCD: the gauge coupling is generated by a fermion determinant, not put in by hand.
- $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}$: the physical QCD scale.
- The bootstrap equation: the MM equation inverted and iterated from $W=1$ (I.6).
- RG: the renormalization group running of $\lambda$.
- Casimir energy correction: $-\pi/(12R)$ in the quark potential.
- $R$: the quark-antiquark separation.

## Derivation

### Step 1 — The induced coupling $\lambda\propto Z[1]^2$ (p108-p109)

The source proposes an MM equation from the surface determinant and identifies its coupling schematically as

$$
\lambda \propto Z[1]^2,
$$

Here $Z[1]$ denotes the source normalization on its reference loop. Defining that determinant and its reference limit requires a regulator and counterterms. The proposal relates a coupling to this quantity; it does not remove the choice of normalization or prove that the resulting theory is QCD. IV.6 leaves the complete contact matching unresolved.

*What is being used:* the MM recovery (IV.6). **Status: Source reconstruction (the proportionality); `slide claim` (the identification as "induced QCD").**

### Step 2 — The tuning $Z[1]^2\to 0$ as $m\to\infty$ (p109)

The source proposes a tuning $Z[1]^2\to0$ as the auxiliary mass tends to infinity. This is a renormalization condition, not a consequence of decoupling alone. A finite massive determinant can grow as a power of the mass; IV.1 gives an explicit example. Its vacuum normalization and counterterms determine the renormalized limit.

Identifying the resulting small coupling with an asymptotically free Yang–Mills coupling requires a perturbative matching calculation. That identification remains conditional on the contact coefficient and determinant construction.

### Step 3 — The bootstrap reproduces planar Feynman graphs (p109)

If the full MM equation and its normalization are recovered, as proposed in IV.6, the source then invokes the bootstrap of I.6: invert $\partial_\mu$ to get a Brownian path integral, iterate from $W=1$, and reproduce the planar Feynman graphs with gluon propagators and Faddeev-Popov ghosts (p109, figure — consult the source PDF). The slide (p109) states: "Iterating this equation systematically with 'glassed windows' ($W\to 1$) flawlessly reproduces the standard planar Feynman graphs, complete with gluon propagators and Faddeev-Popov ghosts."

The "glassed windows" refer to the frame diagrams of I.6: the iteration produces planar trees with windows that are "glassed" by Wilson-loop factors $W$, and in the $W\to 1$ limit (the perturbative vacuum), the windows are trivial and the graphs are the standard perturbative planar diagrams.

*What is being used:* the bootstrap (I.6). **Status: `slide claim` (the claim that the bootstrap "flawlessly reproduces" all planar graphs — inherited from I.6, where it was also `slide claim`).**

### Step 4 — The $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}$ formula (p109, eq (100))

For pure Yang–Mills in the planar limit, suppose the 't Hooft coupling obeys
$$
\mu\frac{d\lambda}{d\mu}=-b_0\lambda^2-b_1\lambda^3+O(\lambda^4),
\quad b_0=\frac{11}{24\pi^2},\quad b_1=\frac{17}{192\pi^4}.
$$
Expand the reciprocal:
$$
\frac{d\log\mu}{d\lambda}
=-\frac1{b_0\lambda^2}+\frac{b_1}{b_0^2\lambda}+O(1).
$$
Integration gives $\log(\mu/\Lambda)=1/(b_0\lambda)+(b_1/b_0^2)\log(b_0\lambda)+O(\lambda)$, so
$$
\Lambda=\mu\,(b_0\lambda)^{-51/121}
\exp\left[-\frac{24\pi^2}{11\lambda}\right][1+O(\lambda)].
$$
The power is negative. The constant $b_0^{-51/121}$ is conventionally absorbed into the definition of $\Lambda$ when comparing with slide 109. This is a two-loop asymptotic relation, not an exact formula for arbitrary coupling. Setting $\mu=m$ is the source's proposed UV matching, which still needs the induced construction to realize the stated beta function.

### Step 5 — The Casimir energy correction from Elves, not string vibrations (p110)

The summary (p110) makes a striking physical claim: the **Casimir energy correction** $-\pi/(12R)$ in the quark potential (the $1/R$ correction to the linear confining potential $V=\sigma R$) comes from the *zero-point fluctuations of two components of elves*, not from two transverse components of a vibrating string.

For a long open bosonic string in four spacetime dimensions, two massless transverse modes give the familiar model correction $-\pi/(12R)$ under the usual boundary assumptions. The source proposes a fermionic origin for a matching term. Two Majorana labels do not by themselves establish the coefficient: the spectrum, spin structure, boundary conditions, and effective central charge determine the Casimir energy. This course does not derive the asserted equality from component counting.

The slide (p110) states: "Replacement of vibrations by elves is not an arbitrary choice of string model-builder; this is a necessity dictated by the topology of planar graphs and the instability of fluctuating surfaces in 4 dimensions."

*What is being used:* the Casimir effect and the identification of the degrees of freedom. **Status: `slide claim` / `interpretive` (the physical identification).**

### Step 6 — Summary of Lecture IV (p110)

The source proposes a chain from a surface Majorana theory through a planar cancellation and contact limit to induced Yang–Mills dynamics. The course has checked finite Grassmann identities, a normalized radial contact model, and the two-loop scale formula. Those checks do not establish the entire chain.

The unresolved inputs include the geometric tensor in III.5, the regulated operator and boundary domain, full configuration cancellation, and matching of the actual contact coefficient. The source's identification of the Casimir term with its auxiliary fermions is likewise model-dependent; the displayed $1/R$ term is a potential energy correction, and its derivative is a force.

## Worked laboratory: the exponent ratio

Using the beta coefficients in the derivation,
$$
\frac{b_1}{b_0^2}
=\frac{17}{192\pi^4}\frac{576\pi^4}{121}
=\frac{51}{121}.
$$
The positive ratio enters with a negative power in $\Lambda/\mu$. Differentiating the displayed two-loop asymptotic expression with the truncated beta function cancels the constant and linear terms in $\lambda$; the remaining error begins at the next stated order. This check detects the sign independently of the slide transcription.

## Common Traps

- **Reading "induced QCD" as a proven result.** It is `slide claim` / `needs external verification`. The mechanism (coupling from determinant, decoupling, asymptotic freedom) is described, but the full proof — that the Elfin theory *is* planar QCD, not just a theory with the same MM equation — is not in the PDF.

- **Confusing an induced gauge kinetic term with the Higgs mechanism.** The source proposes a fermion determinant that supplies gauge dynamics. In the usual Higgs mechanism, a scalar expectation value in a covariant kinetic term supplies gauge-boson mass terms. A scalar determinant alone is not its definition.

- **Treating the $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}$ formula as a prediction.** It is the *standard* asymptotic-freedom formula with $m$ identified as the UV cutoff. The formula itself is `standard`; the identification of $m$ with the Elfin mass is `slide claim`. The formula does not *predict* $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}$ — it relates it to $m$ and $\lambda$, which are parameters of the Elfin theory.

- **Reading "Elves, not vibrations" as a minor point.** It is a *fundamental* claim about the nature of the QCD string: the string does not vibrate, and the Casimir energy correction (a standard prediction of string models) comes from fermions, not from vibrational modes. This is what makes the theory a "rigid string" rather than a Nambu-Goto string.

## Problem set

1. **Classroom core.** Evaluate $b_1/b_0^2$.

2. **Self-study calculation.** Integrate $d\log\mu/d\lambda=-1/(b_0\lambda^2)+b_1/(b_0^2\lambda)$.

3. **Self-study interpretation.** What does the two-loop calculation establish about the induced surface theory?

4. **Research extension.** Specify the renormalization condition on $Z[1]$.

## Answer checkpoints

1. It is $51/121$.

2. The antiderivative is $1/(b_0\lambda)+(b_1/b_0^2)\log\lambda$ plus a constant.

3. It establishes the scale relation if that theory matches the stated beta function. It does not itself prove the matching.

4. Completion: give its regulator, vacuum normalization, mass dependence, and perturbative coupling match. Decoupling rhetoric alone does not determine the determinant limit.
