# Holography through Quantum Information and Operator Algebras

*Written by AI assistants under the scientific and pedagogical supervision of Marcelo S. Guimarães; see [[ai-authorship]] for the division of labour and the models involved.*

How can the observables of a quantum theory encode a spacetime? We begin with encodings, restricted access, and recoverable observables in small quantum systems. Their calculations motivate operator algebras and modular structure. We then introduce continuum QFT, the geometric dictionary, and selected gravitational models.

The course distinguishes exact finite constructions, stated continuum theorems, and semiclassical gravitational results. Students repeatedly identify the additional input needed in passing from one level to the next.

## Audience and prerequisites

The opening block assumes tensor products, density matrices, Hermitian operators, and basic probability. Schmidt decomposition, partial trace, quantum channels, and entropy are reviewed where used. No prior operator-algebra or error-correction course is required.

Later sections require free QFT and basic general relativity. The needed CFT, AdS, and modular constructions are developed within the course. String theory and completion of the parallel AQFT course are not prerequisites.

## Learning outcomes

Students should be able to:

1. Specify accessible observables and distinguish an algebra, its commutant, and its center.
2. Build and test an erasure decoder, including reference entanglement.
3. Calculate finite modular data and identify when modular flow is geometric.
4. Derive entropy and relative-entropy identities in algebraic codes.
5. Distinguish regulator removal, split inclusions, and a state-adapted large-$N$ limit.
6. Complete a scalar source–response calculation and an AdS interval-entropy calculation.
7. Explain wedge reconstruction and JLMS within a specified code and approximation.
8. Compare finite Page models with a calculated gravitational entropy saddle.
9. Use a half-sided modular inclusion to understand an affine translation, without assuming it already determines a full spacetime.

## How to use the notes

All listed notes are written drafts. Lectures 8–32 are master dossiers at the AQFT standard, fuller than a single classroom run, and the opening dossier (Lectures 1–7) follows the same structure with its own reading map. Their proposed teaching routes remain provisional.

[[course-instructor-guide|The instructor guide]] identifies the classroom core, self-study work, and research extensions for every unit. A theorem may be stated with hypotheses when its full proof belongs in an AQFT or mathematical methods course. An exercise does not replace a calculation claimed to be derived in the text.

The recurrent questions are: what is the system, what is the state, which algebra is accessible, what is calculated, and what additional assumption supports a geometric interpretation? The [[courses/ads-cft-course/conventions|conventions]] and [[course-reading-map|source map]] support that progression.

## Scope choices

The final algebraic route is modular inclusions and emergent time. Crossed-product entropy and traversability remain further-reading options. Long conformal-block calculations, a catalogue of Witten diagrams, and Wilson-loop technology are optional extensions.

The JT/bath sequence contains a static analytic island endpoint and a two-sided thermal entropy plateau. It does not claim to solve a complete evaporating geometry. The finite Page models precede that calculation so students can separate unitarity, typicality, and gravitational saddle assumptions.

## Problems and projects

Each note includes problems with answers or answer checkpoints. Core exercises use the note's prerequisites; self-study exercises consolidate the derivation; extensions declare their additional demands. The project guide offers early finite-model projects, and [[lecture-32-course-synthesis|Lecture 32]] adds field, scalar-dictionary, island, and modular projects.

The course is a completed written draft, not a classroom-tested release. See the validation record for what has been checked.
